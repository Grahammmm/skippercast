# California seabed coverage and promotion gate

SkipperCast can browse the California coast, but precise, species-specific fishing grounds require finer evidence than statewide map visibility. Do not label a catalog-linked survey, a rectangular metadata bound, or a filename as a target.

The automated source inventory reads the [USGS California State Waters Map Series catalog](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/) and the [NOAA NCEI survey catalog](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/bathymetry/hydrographic-surveys). Its first pass missed newer USGS DOI releases; that discovery path is now separate and explicit. At the 2026-09-23 audit, 20 older map-block catalogs and 18 newer DOI releases were indexed. The older pages linked 35 original XML records and all 35 original grids were opened. The newer releases yielded 36 original XML records, with 28 of 30 selected bathymetry or seafloor-character grids opened; two large bathymetry archives exceeded the bounded download. Those are historical, discontinuous surveys, not whole-coast coverage. NOAA's separate discovery inventory linked 427 original BAG files across 183 survey leads. All 427 links answered an HTTP HEAD check; 198 of 199 selected BAGs at or below 20 MB opened, with 173 passing the strict MLLW/product-uncertainty metadata adapter. A survey lead may cover a harbor or bay rather than outer-coast fishing grounds: a BAG linked to the broad Point Reyes–Pigeon sector was titled “San Francisco Bay” in its native metadata.

The USGS San Gregorio character metadata is a useful example of evidence quality: it documents 2 m pixels, video-supervised classification and substrate classes including fine/medium smooth sediment, mixed sediment and rock, rugose rock and boulder, and coarser mobile sediment. [Original XML](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/OffshoreSanGregorio/metadata/SeafloorCharacter_OffshoreSanGregorio_metadata.xml). Its classification is still historical and does not establish a present fish aggregation or a navigable route.

The optional map layer contains 1,580 generalized class-3 hard-bottom outlines from the inspected older blocks and newer DOI releases. It uses original categorical pixels, 20 m display generalization, and cuts polygons by the September 23 statewide CDFW MPA snapshot. The layer loads one coast at a time on mobile, remains absent on the Mendocino coast where these sources yielded no reviewed patches, and cannot be exported as fishing waypoints. The MPA cut date and original source URLs remain attached to features. NOAA BAG depth and product uncertainty have **not** yet been joined to those polygons, so no new rockfish/lingcod point is promoted from this layer. The 199 small-file BAG scan is a bounded subset of 427 original links; coarse statewide BAG overviews with sparse valid cells cannot be mistaken for 1–4 m local survey coverage.

A second optional layer uses [CDFW's predicted nearshore benthic substrate dataset ds3091](https://filelib.wildlife.ca.gov/public/BDB/GIS/BIOS/metadata/ds3091.html). Its ArcGIS image service publishes class 1 as “Hard” and class 2 as “Soft.” On September 23, the 40 m LERC tile audit decoded all 2,870 tiles intersecting our 19 coastal browse sectors, with zero failed tiles. After retaining broad hard-class components of at least 0.16 km² and cutting current MPA polygons with a conservative 100 m display buffer, it produced 96 Northern, 51 Mendocino, 98 San Francisco, 180 Central, and 287 Southern context patches. CDFW explicitly describes this classification as a **rugosity proxy**, with variable source resolution and interpolated shallow gaps. These broad predictions fill coastwide context gaps, especially Mendocino, but are not verified rock piles, exact boundaries, depth-qualified fishing locations or catch predictions. The map keeps the predicted layer visually distinct from original-grid USGS survey classes and leaves both off by default.

The reusable promotion sequence for every new coast sector is:

1. Discover official product URLs, dates, rights and download health. Publish explicit gaps and failures.
2. Read original XML and native raster/BAG, record acquisition date, geographic bounds, raster footprint and no-data pixels, horizontal and vertical datum, resolution and uncertainty. Do not infer coverage from a block name or bounding box.
3. Extract seabed classes, depth and terrain metrics at the native resolution; validate against observations where possible. Keep original file hash, metadata hash and provenance on each derived feature.
4. Apply the appropriate region-specific species habitat model, depth rules, season, MPA geometry, closures, security areas and export restrictions. An unverified rule leaves the target pending.
5. Review sample polygons and point placement before publication. Separate historical habitat suitability from dynamic temperature, current and weather evidence; show confidence and freshness on the map.

The daily [data pipeline](../.github/workflows/daily-data.yml) refreshes discovery and XML metadata inventories about monthly and republishes health to the public data branch. Native raster downloads and derived context are explicit review builds with pinned file hashes, not every-day web requests. The inventories do **not** automatically promote fishing spots. This boundary prevents stale or mislocated government datasets from silently becoming “best spots” when expanding statewide.

The [NOAA National Bathymetric Source](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/learn/nbs.html) Modeling tile scheme is an additional candidate source for the Central Coast gaps. It is a **test-and-evaluation** product. Unlike the national-datum BlueTopo display product, the inspected Modeling GeoTIFFs declare MLLW and carry elevation, uncertainty, and a contributor raster with a source/date/coverage attribute table. `scripts/audit_nbs_modeling_tile.py` validates exact NOAA scheme checksums and screens actual 25–200 ft pixels for measured contributors, survey year, and uncertainty. The [pinned Point Sur review](../dist/data/nbs-modeling-BH44P5CD-review.json) found 1,340,369 pixels in that depth range, but only 2,605 with measured-contributor flags, none surveyed since 1990, and **zero** passing the complete ≤1 m uncertainty screen. Additional sampled Big Sur and San Simeon tiles (`BH44R5CB`, `BH44R5CC`, `BH4525C2`, `BH4545C2`) likewise pass zero. The last tile does contain 209,265 measured 2015 survey pixels in range, but their product uncertainty is at least 1.16 m. Many other apparent fine-grid depths come from interpolated 1930s soundings. A 4 m tile resolution is not 4 m measured seabed evidence. These five samples do not establish the status of every Central Coast tile. This source currently creates no fishing points; wider tile-by-tile review and separate substrate, hazard, MPA, access, and species-rule checks remain necessary.

The [monthly substrate review](../.github/workflows/monthly-substrate.yml) rechecks all CDFW ds3091 overview tiles and rebuilds MPA-screened candidate context against the latest public boundary snapshot. It uploads an audit and review layers without automatically changing the public atlas. Promotion requires comparing changed geometry and source classes, rerunning the MPA check, and publishing a reviewed commit.

The same monthly review now downloads the hash-pinned [NBS Modeling tile scheme](../catalog/nbs-modeling-sample.json), checks the public NOAA listing for a newer scheme, and reruns the 19-sector rocky-camera source sample against original GeoTIFFs, contributor tables and the current CDFW MPA snapshot. A changed scheme is reported as needing review; it does not silently replace the pin or publish fishing points. Exact NOAA raster/table checksum changes fail the review. This is a source-health pipeline rather than an automatic habitat or catch model.

The repeatable [native-depth overlap screen](../scripts/assess_native_depth_overlap.py) prioritizes the next manual survey work. In the September 23 bounded NOAA sample, 10 MLLW/product-uncertainty BAG products intersected the broad CDFW predicted class at 25–200 ft, while only four also intersected the stronger USGS original seafloor-character class. H11951, H11732, H11733 and H12112 are the original-class survey IDs for native-cell, habitat and local-access review. H11951 showed a large USGS-class overlap but very little CDFW predicted-class overlap, which is exactly why the predicted mosaic must not replace local survey evidence. The 4 m screen is a lead count, not a qualified fishing point or a claim that every valid cell is reef. The remaining larger BAGs and missing coastal reaches still need native review.

The September 24 [31-file original variable-resolution BAG audit](../dist/data/noaa-vr-native-depth-review.json) gives a more explicit mainland gap map. Within the bounded ≤100 MB file set, eligible 1–4 m original cells were found in the Fort Bragg–Point Arena, North Mendocino, Point Reyes–Pigeon, Bodega–Point Reyes, San Diego–border, Orange–San Diego, Los Angeles–Orange and Point Conception–Ventura browse sectors. Five consecutive Central Coast sectors from Pigeon Point through Cambria–Morro have **zero eligible original cells in this audit**; that is a source-coverage gap, not proof of flat or fishless bottom. The audit also reports 1.48 million depth/uncertainty-eligible H13555 cells in the Santa Rosa review envelope. A separate full-footprint original BAG plus historical substrate and closure screen yielded only two flat C-grade patches near 118–122 ft; both remain [held source leads](../catalog/noaa-survey-lead-holds.json), not rockfish or lingcod targets. The [H13555 final report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H12001-H14000/H13555/DR/H13555_DR.pdf) lists no shoals or hazardous features, but also no bottom samples. H13084 around San Miguel has deeper survey hazards and mobile sand-wave concerns recorded in the same hold registry.

Farther south, [NOAA H11877 off San Elijo Lagoon](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/nos/H10001-H12000/H11877.html) is another accessible original BAG lead. Its [final survey report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H10001-H12000/H11877/DR/H11877.pdf) says 2008 chart agreement was generally good, identifies steep slopes, and reports no assigned bottom samples or dangers to navigation. Native depth availability and a steep slope alone do not establish a hard-bottom fishing area. The next San Diego review must intersect native cells with independently supported substrate, present closures and a current chart before considering a waypoint.
