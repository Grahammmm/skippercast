# California seabed coverage and promotion gate

SkipperCast can browse the California coast, but precise, species-specific fishing grounds require finer evidence than statewide map visibility. Do not label a catalog-linked survey, a rectangular metadata bound, or a filename as a target.

The automated source inventory reads the [USGS California State Waters Map Series catalog](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/) and the [NOAA NCEI survey catalog](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/bathymetry/hydrographic-surveys). Its first pass missed newer USGS DOI releases; that discovery path is now separate and explicit. At the 2026-09-23 audit, 20 older map-block catalogs and 18 newer DOI releases were indexed. The older pages linked 35 original XML records and all 35 original grids were opened. The newer releases yielded 36 original XML records, with 28 of 30 selected bathymetry or seafloor-character grids opened; two large bathymetry archives exceeded the bounded download. Those are historical, discontinuous surveys, not whole-coast coverage. NOAA's separate discovery inventory linked 427 original BAG files across 183 survey leads. All 427 links answered an HTTP HEAD check; 198 of 199 selected BAGs at or below 20 MB opened, with 173 passing the strict MLLW/product-uncertainty metadata adapter. A survey lead may cover a harbor or bay rather than outer-coast fishing grounds: a BAG linked to the broad Point Reyes–Pigeon sector was titled “San Francisco Bay” in its native metadata.

The USGS San Gregorio character metadata is a useful example of evidence quality: it documents 2 m pixels, video-supervised classification and substrate classes including fine/medium smooth sediment, mixed sediment and rock, rugose rock and boulder, and coarser mobile sediment. [Original XML](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/OffshoreSanGregorio/metadata/SeafloorCharacter_OffshoreSanGregorio_metadata.xml). Its classification is still historical and does not establish a present fish aggregation or a navigable route.

The optional map layer contains 1,580 generalized class-3 hard-bottom outlines from the inspected older blocks and newer DOI releases. It uses original categorical pixels, 20 m display generalization, and cuts polygons by the September 23 statewide CDFW MPA snapshot. The layer loads one coast at a time on mobile, remains absent on the Mendocino coast where these sources yielded no reviewed patches, and cannot be exported as fishing waypoints. The MPA cut date and original source URLs remain attached to features. NOAA BAG depth and product uncertainty have **not** yet been joined to those polygons, so no new rockfish/lingcod point is promoted from this layer. The 199 small-file BAG scan is a bounded subset of 427 original links; coarse statewide BAG overviews with sparse valid cells cannot be mistaken for 1–4 m local survey coverage.

The reusable promotion sequence for every new coast sector is:

1. Discover official product URLs, dates, rights and download health. Publish explicit gaps and failures.
2. Read original XML and native raster/BAG, record acquisition date, geographic bounds, raster footprint and no-data pixels, horizontal and vertical datum, resolution and uncertainty. Do not infer coverage from a block name or bounding box.
3. Extract seabed classes, depth and terrain metrics at the native resolution; validate against observations where possible. Keep original file hash, metadata hash and provenance on each derived feature.
4. Apply the appropriate region-specific species habitat model, depth rules, season, MPA geometry, closures, security areas and export restrictions. An unverified rule leaves the target pending.
5. Review sample polygons and point placement before publication. Separate historical habitat suitability from dynamic temperature, current and weather evidence; show confidence and freshness on the map.

The daily [data pipeline](../.github/workflows/daily-data.yml) refreshes discovery and XML metadata inventories about monthly and republishes health to the public data branch. Native raster downloads and derived context are explicit review builds with pinned file hashes, not every-day web requests. The inventories do **not** automatically promote fishing spots. This boundary prevents stale or mislocated government datasets from silently becoming “best spots” when expanding statewide.
