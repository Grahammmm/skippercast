# San Pedro Shelf original habitat review

The Los Angeles–Orange atlas sector has a newly inspected source: [USGS Data Series 552](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/552/). Its original 4 m inner-shelf and 16 m outer-shelf categorical rasters distinguish historical rugose rock, mixed bottom and sediments. The companion 2004 camera file reports observed bottom material. The [catalog candidate](../catalog/candidates/usgs-ds552-san-pedro.json) records original URLs, SHA-256 digests, coverage and limits.

On September 25, 2026 the pinned source audit counted **197,401 inner-shelf 4 m rugose-rock cells**. There were **151 connected rugose-rock components of at least 2,500 m²**; **24 of these** contained at least one camera record explicitly observing rock, boulder or cobble. Camera observations sampled on the rock class were hard-bottom positive in 102 of 148 records. These are correlated historical samples, not 102 independent reefs or catch reports. The outer 16 m raster is much coarser and is retained as background source evidence.

The audit produces an **unpublished review queue**, not map spots. Its component center is only a bounding-box reference. No depth or bottom uncertainty is stored in these character pixels. Before any individual area can appear as a fishing target, the pipeline must join its exact footprint to original MLLW depth and uncertainty, current CDFW MPAs and federal closures, current charted dangers, route/access constraints, date-specific fishing regulations, and current conditions. It must also avoid counting a camera transect twice as independent support. The source is static and old enough that a current fish score needs separate evidence.

Run `python scripts/audit_usgs_ds552_san_pedro.py --cache var/review/usgs-ds552 --output var/review/usgs-ds552-san-pedro-review.json` after downloading the three original ZIPs named in the script. The monthly substrate workflow repeats this download and source-pinned audit, and uploads its result as a private CI artifact for review. A changed archive fails closed until its publication and transformation are checked. The public atlas still shows **zero newly qualified targets** from this review.
