# San Pedro Shelf original habitat review

The Los Angeles–Orange atlas sector has a newly inspected source: [USGS Data Series 552](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/552/). Its original 4 m inner-shelf and 16 m outer-shelf categorical rasters distinguish historical rugose rock, mixed bottom and sediments. The companion 2004 camera file reports observed bottom material. The [catalog candidate](../catalog/candidates/usgs-ds552-san-pedro.json) records original URLs, SHA-256 digests, coverage and limits.

On September 25, 2026 the pinned source audit counted **197,401 inner-shelf 4 m rugose-rock cells**. There were **151 connected rugose-rock components of at least 2,500 m²**; **24 of these** contained at least one camera record explicitly observing rock, boulder or cobble. Camera observations sampled on the rock class were hard-bottom positive in 102 of 148 records. These are correlated historical samples, not 102 independent reefs or catch reports. The outer 16 m raster is much coarser and is retained as background source evidence.

The companion [USGS Open-File Report 2004-1221](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1221/) supplies original 4 m bathymetry corrected to MLLW. Three SHA-pinned grids cover all **11,732 rugose-rock class cells** in the 24 review components; every sampled cell falls within **25–200 ft MLLW**. The measured range across these components is **61.7–138.5 ft**, with individual component ranges recorded in the unpublished review. At the overlapping grid seams, the greatest difference between source grids is **1.81 m**. The publisher warns of data-collection and processing artifacts, and these grids do not supply a per-cell product-uncertainty band. Thus the full historical depth band is supported, but the sites are still not fishing-qualified.

The audit produces an **unpublished review queue**, not map spots. Its component center is only a bounding-box reference. Before any individual area can appear as a fishing target, the pipeline must review uncertainty and source artifacts, current CDFW MPAs and federal closures, current charted dangers, route/access constraints, date-specific fishing regulations, and current conditions. It must also avoid counting a camera transect twice as independent support. The source is static and old enough that a current fish score needs separate evidence.

The pipeline now reconstructs the **exact 4 m rock-cell outlines** from the pinned original raster and compares them to fresh complete CDFW MPA and NOAA federal groundfish area polygons with a 100 m review buffer. In the September 25, 2026 snapshot, **0 of 24** outlines intersected that buffer. This is a dated geometry screen only: current legal boundaries and specific fishing rules still control, and it does not imply safe navigation or access.

A separate bounded [NOAA ENC Direct](https://encdirect.noaa.gov/) review checked all 18 required danger layers across harbor, approach and coastal scales on September 25, 2026. **Two of 24** outlines intersect charted obstruction areas and points within the 100 m review buffer; they are held. The nearest selected charted danger to any of the other 22 outlines is **394.2 m** in this query. ENC Direct is not a complete chart or a navigation clearance, and the absence of a selected danger feature near an outline does not establish a safe route.

The [NOAA Fisheries current depth-based boundary archive](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/resource/data/depth-based-boundary-lines-west-coast) includes the official 50-fathom coordinate file effective January 1, 2025. A September 25, 2026 screen of each exact rock outline places **all 24 shoreward** of that coordinate line, at least **2.86 km** from it. The 50-fathom line is a legal coordinate boundary and is **not** inferred from the 61.7–138.5 ft historical soundings. The [CDFW Southern Management Area rules](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Fishing-Map/Southern) distinguish July–September shoreward access from October–December seaward access for the relevant groundfish groups; therefore this research queue cannot be presented as seasonless rockfish or lingcod fishing locations. The current rule, in-season changes and controlling federal coordinates must be checked again for a trip date. NOAA warns that the Federal Register controls if its downloadable coordinates disagree.

Run `python scripts/audit_usgs_ds552_san_pedro.py --cache var/review/usgs-ds552 --depth-cache var/review/usgs-of2004-1221 --output var/review/usgs-ds552-san-pedro-review.json` after downloading the six original archives named in the script. The monthly substrate workflow repeats the download, full-cell depth join, fresh closure review, bounded NOAA ENC danger screen and NOAA 50-fathom coordinate comparison, then uploads dated receipts as private CI artifacts. A changed archive fails closed until its publication and transformation are checked. The public atlas still shows **zero newly qualified targets** from this review.
