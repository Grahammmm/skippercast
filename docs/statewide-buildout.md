# California coast buildout ledger

SkipperCast now divides the outer coast into the five CDFW ocean browsing regions and **19 smaller discovery sectors**. The sector boundaries are approximate latitude bands for organizing source work. They are not legal lines, navigable areas, fishing grounds or proof of survey coverage. San Francisco Bay and offshore islands need their own local legal/habitat treatment; the existing Southern California package has island focus areas but only a qualified subset of bottom.

`catalog/coastal-sectors.json` is the reviewed sector index. `src/skippercast/platform/sectors.py` verifies that the bands meet without gaps or overlaps and publishes `dist/data/coastal-sectors.json`. A sector records the number of **already published point candidates**, but never calls its whole rectangle mapped. The shared coastal guide can zoom to each sector and shows source-status text. The Northern, Mendocino and San Francisco coast groups still have no detailed regional fishing package; selecting them must not borrow Morro Bay spots or forecast samples. The coastwide Conditions view now requests a fresh model grid near the actual map center for each sector; it is a planning outlook, not a detailed regional trip assessment.

## Survey discovery is running, qualification is separate

### Original USGS video groundtruth

The [USGS DS 781 video-observation catalog](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/video_observations/data_catalog_video_observations.html) provides 12 original georeferenced camera-log ZIPs spanning the California outer coast. `catalog/usgs-video-cruises.json` pins each archive URL and SHA-256. `scripts/audit_usgs_video_observations.py` downloads bounded originals, validates hashes and WGS84 geometry, normalizes cruise-specific field names, and reports historical bottom, rock/boulder/cobble, rockfish and lingcod observation counts by the 19 discovery sectors in `dist/data/usgs-video-observation-audit.json`. The monthly substrate workflow reruns this audit and uploads the result for review. A changed archive hash fails the audit pending source review; it cannot silently change the public map.

The first complete pass examined **27,987 original log records** across the 12 archives, of which **23,005** contain a bottom observation. Records are survey-transect observations, not independent uniformly sampled sites or fish abundance. Some cruise schemas omit species fields altogether; the audit includes per-sector field coverage so a missing value is not treated as a negative observation. USGS describes horizontal camera-position accuracy as **highly variable, on the order of 10 m**, and says the data are not for navigation. This original evidence is **not a fishing-spot or catch-success layer**. Before a video record can support a specific habitat outline, a reviewer must check its original metadata, date, camera positioning, actual spatial overlap, local bottom class and legal screen. In particular, the 2010 C210NC records provide **zero bottom observations within 250 m of the 121 Cape Mendocino native-depth display outlines**, so none of those outlines is labeled video verified. This is an explicit evidence gap, not a reason to extrapolate the nearby observations.

`scripts/audit_usgs_native_overlap.py` cross-checks all 12 pinned video archives against the four published native NOAA/USGS hard-bottom context layers. It withholds observations closer than 25 m to a generalized polygon edge to account conservatively for the variable camera position and display simplification. Of **287** context outlines, **one** Point Conception outline has interior historical camera evidence: ten annotated windows on **one** C0212SC transect dated August 22, 2012, nine labeled rock and one sand. This corroborates **mixed bottom along that transect**, not a uniform reef, fish presence, catch quality, current legal access or a waypoint. Three San Francisco-coast camera windows touch display outlines but are within the edge hold and are not attributed to a specific patch. The monthly review publishes the full cross-check as an artifact; a reviewed public receipt is `dist/data/usgs-video-native-overlap.json`.

The [statewide regular-BAG/camera review](../dist/data/noaa-statewide-regular-camera-review.json) joins the original rocky USGS windows to each exact, hash-pinned fine regular NOAA BAG file, rather than counting survey bounding boxes or treating multiple BAG resolutions as independent surveys. The September 24 run screened **84 exact file/cruise pairs** with actual rocky camera coordinates in their survey bounds. Thirty-four pairs, representing **15 distinct surveys**, contained at least one camera window with a measured 25–200 ft MLLW cell, supplied product uncertainty at most 1 m, a 2 m planning depth margin and at least 90% locally qualified cells within 25 m. Five pairs remain held by unreviewed embedded vertical datums. Results span eight of the 19 approximate mainland browse sectors; the other sectors are **not** therefore soft or fishless. The strongest count cluster is Bodega–Point Reyes, but counts are historical windows, often repeated across overlapping BAG files, not sites or catch rates. None enters fishing scores, map targets or exports. Each prospective area still needs a separately reviewed substrate footprint, descriptive-report hazards, current chart and legal/closure review.

The [statewide NOAA NBS Modeling camera-tile sample](../dist/data/nbs-statewide-camera-tile-review.json) adds an independent source-*delivery* check, not an independent seabed measurement when it reuses the same NOAA surveys. The reproducible selector chooses one available 2–4 m tile envelope per sector with the most historical USGS rocky-camera windows, then validates NOAA's exact GeoTIFF and contributor-table hashes. All **19 sectors** had a selected tile and all 19 original raster/table pairs opened. Native MLLW cells are screened for a measured survey contributor dated 1990 or later, 25–200 ft depth, supplied uncertainty at most 1 m and the same 2 m depth margin used by the BAG pipeline. Eleven sampled sectors contain at least one passing pixel; seven have at least one historical rocky camera window with a passing center and at least 90% passing cells in a 25 m circle outside the refreshed CDFW MPA geometry. Those seven sectors are Del Norte, Crescent City–Humboldt, Humboldt–Cape Mendocino, Fort Bragg–Point Arena, Point Arena–Bodega, Bodega–Point Reyes and Orange County–San Diego. The **438 qualifying camera windows are correlated observations along historical transects, not 438 independent reefs or fishing spots**. Another 208 sampled windows were held because their 25 m neighborhoods intersect an MPA. A zero in the one-tile sample does not establish an empty sector; Monterey, Big Sur and other gaps need wider native-product searches. This NBS source is test-and-evaluation, not a navigation chart, and none of these pixels or windows enters targets or exports without substrate-footprint, hazards, federal-area, route and current local-rule review.

The official [NOAA NCEI NOS survey-footprint service](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/web_mercator/nos_hydro_dynamic/MapServer) provides intersecting surveys with BAG products. `scripts/discover_noaa_surveys.py` queries every sector, validates complete pages, records exact request URLs, retrieval time and response SHA-256, and retains the previous dated result on a failed query. The existing daily workflow runs it when the last **complete** scan is 30 days old, publishes `survey-discovery.json` on the `data` branch and reports degraded coverage. A saved snapshot is a dated fallback. The first complete scan on September 23, 2026 found **183 distinct survey IDs** across the 19 sectors; one survey may intersect several sectors. This is a catalog lead count, not 183 usable high-resolution grids.

To promote one lead, the add-region skill must inspect its original BAG and descriptive report, acquisition date, local footprint and native cells; confirm horizontal/vertical reference, depth, product uncertainty, masks, resolution and rights; then bind any separately verified substrate. The survey compiler checks the full footprint against current MPA/groundfish exclusions and the region's depth allowance. NOAA's survey polygon alone cannot identify rocks, fish or safe passage. [USGS California Seafloor Mapping Program](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/pcmsc/science/california-seafloor-mapping-program) map blocks and groundtruth are a second discovery path, with each product's own terms and scale reviewed. The [NOAA Bathymetric Data Viewer](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/maps/bathymetry/) is another official access path, not an independent measurement when it serves the same survey.

`scripts/audit_noaa_survey_products.py` checks the original catalog page for every discovered survey, bounds and hashes that response, and identifies direct BAG, descriptive-report and gridded-data links under the same NOAA survey ID. Its first scan found BAG links on all 183 catalog pages and report links on 181. A linked file has **not** been downloaded or qualified by this audit. The existing daily workflow runs the product inventory on a 30-day cycle, retains dated results on a page failure, publishes `survey-products.json`, and reports failures. The coastal guide now lets a reader inspect the dated original product links for a chosen sector. This is a reproducible queue for native-data review, not an automatic point generator.

The first original seafloor-character import is [USGS's Cape Mendocino data release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9U0SUGL). The source provides a 2 m video-supervised class grid and a separate 2 m bathymetry product. Metadata says class 3 represents hard, rugged seabed, with public-domain redistribution and attribution to USGS and CSU Monterey Bay. The adapter pins the original character ZIP hash and publishes **43 generalized historical context polygons** at a 20 m display grid. It omits patches under 20,000 m², so the outlines must not be treated as exact rock edges. This layer is optional on the Northern guide map and cannot enter waypoint export or fish ranking. The companion bathymetry reports an instantaneous-sea-level reference; it has **not** been used for legal depth qualification. [USGS's Humboldt study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20241047) reports that the hard, rugose outcrops are concentrated in the southern study area off Cape Mendocino. No current catch or local legal permission follows from that geological finding.

The [Cape Mendocino original-source coverage audit](../dist/data/noaa-cape-mendocino-vr-source-coverage.json) separately opens three large official NOAA MLLW variable-resolution BAGs: [H11973](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/nos/H10001-H12000/H11973.html), [H11974](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/nos/H10001-H12000/H11974.html), and [H11975](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/nos/H10001-H12000/H11975.html). These require an explicit survey ID, 450 MB bound and one download worker; the routine statewide 100 MB scan is unchanged. H11973's coarse overview box intersects the USGS Cape source but **none of its actual supergrid footprints do**; H11974 also has zero actual overlap. H11975 has 23,074 fine (at most 4 m) supergrid footprints intersecting the original USGS raster extent. This is a footprint lead, not yet a measured depth or bottom claim.

The companion [H11975 original-cell screen](../dist/data/noaa-h11975-cape-mendocino-hard-depth-screen.json) validates the original BAG and USGS hashes, embedded MLLW and product-uncertainty metadata, and native cell orientation against GDAL. It joins USGS class-3 hard/rugose pixels with NOAA cells in 25–200 ft planning depth, a 2 m depth margin and at most 1 m supplied uncertainty. It then excludes a 100 m margin around the complete fresh CDFW MPA and NOAA GEA snapshots, plus 11 historical submerged-rock danger positions transcribed from the [original H11975 descriptive report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H10001-H12000/H11975/DR/H11975.pdf). The dated result retains **12,433,583 cells** in 13,933 fine supergrids after those exclusions. These are *mixed-resolution historical cell counts*, not unique square meters or published fishing locations. The result has no geometry, target rating or export. In particular, the report describes dangers and chart discrepancies; a current chart, route, local species/method rules and present-day fish evidence are still required. [CDFW's Northern rule page](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Fishing-Map/Northern) is an official starting point, not a clearance for every date and coordinate.

The [optional Northern original-cell map layer](../dist/data/cape-mendocino-native-hard-context.geojson) turns the screened H11975 cells into **121 inward research outlines** off Cape Mendocino. `scripts/build_vr_hard_context.py` reruns the entire original-cell screen, requires its counts and hashes to match the pinned receipt, aggregates only accepted cells on a 5 m display grid, erodes the map inward, and cuts complete current MPAs, GEAs and the report's historical rock holds again. An independent geometry check found no MPA/GEA polygon overlap in the published snapshot. The 2,500 m² display threshold omits smaller patches; the largest published outline is about 12,100 m². Each popup reports approximate displayed area and the 5–95% relief of historical native-depth samples in its pre-vector connected component, not a fish or catch score. A polygon's 25–200 ft label is the **screen policy**, not a measured depth range for that individual outline. Generalization, 2008–2009 survey age and still-unreviewed current chart, route, legal method and fish-presence gates keep all 121 shapes out of fishing targets, rankings, drift lines and exports.

### Original-cell Bodega–Bolinas review

`scripts/qualify_regular_bag_hard.py` now intersects **original** 1–2 m NOAA MLLW elevation/uncertainty cells with separately pinned original USGS seafloor-character class-3 pixels for H11732–H11735, H11738, H12109 and H12111. The [NOAA federal-area feed](../dist/data/noaa-federal-areas.json) now pulls every individual GEA, CCA and YRCA layer from the [NOAA Fisheries WCR GIS service](https://maps.fisheries.noaa.gov/server/rest/services/WCR_SFD_GCA/NOAA_Fisheries_West_Coast_Region_Groundfish_Conservation_Area_service/MapServer) with per-layer count and digest checks. The coastal browse chart defaults to GEAs for reef selection; CCA/YRCA boundaries have a separate optional switch, lighter styling and method-specific notes. [CDFW says Amendment 32 removed CCA restrictions for recreational groundfish while retaining them for groundfish trawl fisheries](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Cowcod); a CCA is therefore not presented as a recreational closure. A daily workflow checks and publishes the feed. The source GIS is approximate and [NOAA says 50 CFR 660 controls the exact legal boundary](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/resource/map/groundfish-conservation-areas-maps-and-gis-data-west-coast-region). [NOAA's June 23, 2026 action](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/action/cordell-bank-area-revisions) replaced the old Cordell Bank groundfish conservation area with a new GEA, which the collector requires explicitly.

The hazard report registry is `catalog/noaa-survey-hazards.json`. These holds are review exclusions, not safe approach distances; current ENC and notices remain necessary.
