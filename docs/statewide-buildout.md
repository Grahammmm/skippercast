# California coast buildout ledger

## State-waters map-area acquisition queue

The official [USGS California State Waters Map Series index](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/781/) has been parsed into a [39-map-area source inventory](../catalog/usgs-ds781-source-leads.json). It records 212 **catalog product links**: 34 bathymetry, 41 seafloor-character, 64 habitat and 73 backscatter links. These are links, not distinct inspected surveys, measured footprints, quality-controlled substrate or fishing locations. The [19-sector readiness queue](../dist/data/california-atlas-readiness.json) associates map-area names with broad acquisition sectors; three northernmost/southernmost groups and several Southern California bands have no DS 781 association, so this series does not solve statewide coverage on its own. The catalog inventory explicitly flags the index's shared Arcata/Eureka DOI: the linked release has Arcata-named products but no matching Eureka product, so Eureka is not counted as sourced.

`scripts/discover_usgs_ds781_catalogs.py` rechecks the original index and catalogs each month. Its CI comparison gates on parsed map-area identity and product links, rather than volatile HTML bytes, and saves an unpublished receipt with page hashes. A changed link fails for human review; it never automatically becomes a public map layer. For each useful product the next step is to open the original archive and metadata, inspect actual valid cells, resolution, vertical datum, uncertainty and license, then compare independent substrate evidence, hazards, current MPA geometry and local fishing rules. [CSUMB's current data-library policy](https://csumb.edu/undersea/sfml-data-library/) permits public non-for-profit use with requested acknowledgment but requires express permission for for-profit use and says these data are not for navigation. The original project metadata and each product's terms still need reconciliation before public derivative publication; this inventory itself only publishes official links.

The follow-on [original FGDC metadata triage](../catalog/usgs-ds781-metadata-review.json) opened **72 of 75** linked bathymetry and seafloor-character XML records; the three Eel River links have no metadata URL in the catalog and remain held. Among the 34 bathymetry links, original XML declares NAVD88 for eight and **instantaneous sea level** for two (Arcata and Cape Mendocino); 23 other reviewed bathymetry records leave the structured vertical datum blank, and one Eel River link is missing. These descriptions must not be silently converted to MLLW or called chart depth. Thirty-nine of the 72 reviewed product records explicitly say both *public domain* and *freely redistributable* with source attribution; other records require individual rights review. Some XML spacing fields are implausibly tiny or list multiple values, so the pipeline flags their plausibility and still requires an actual native raster inspection. The monthly workflow compares each original metadata digest and parsed values, holding changes for review. Sector source details display these **metadata leads**, not surveyed area or legal fishing positions.

The first expanded original-grid inspection is [Offshore Aptos](../dist/data/usgs-offshore-aptos-native-audit.json), reproduced with `scripts/inspect_usgs_native_grids.py --block-id OffshoreAptos` and the normal reviewed USGS block/metadata manifests. All **four** original archives passed SHA-256 and native GeoTIFF inspection. The 2 m bathymetry has **45,280,692** valid source-elevation pixels; its paired 2 m character grid has **44,458,681** valid classified pixels. The original raster value table was opened and its counts matched every native class value; **156,741 pixels** are specifically labeled video-supervised rugose rock/boulder across the original depth and slope zones. That is a source class, not 156,741 independent reefs, fish observations, or within-200-ft chart-depth cells. The associated [candidate record](../catalog/candidates/usgs-offshore-aptos-original-grids.json) remains research-only while NAVD88-to-MLLW conversion, product uncertainty, valid-cell spatial overlap, MPAs, chart hazards, access, species rules, and routes are checked. It adds no ranked or exportable fishing mark.

The reusable [original-rock/NOAA MLLW overlap screen](../dist/data/usgs-offshore-aptos-noaa-mllw-overlap-review.json) then joined the Aptos value-table rock codes **3, 13 and 53** to all **nine** intersecting, checksum-verified NOAA Modeling tiles. Seven tiles contained original rock-class pixels at NOAA cell centers, but **none of the nine had a pixel passing the measured 25–200 ft MLLW, recent-survey and product-uncertainty screen**, so the strict overlap is zero. The nearby original NOAA F00600 BAG is finer, but the [public native-depth review](../dist/data/noaa-regular-native-depth-review.json) gives it zero cells passing this 25–200 ft and product-uncertainty screen. These are bounded negative source results, not proof that the whole Aptos coast lacks fishable rock. NOAA Modeling is a test-and-evaluation product; a different original survey, defensible datum transformation with uncertainty, and the legal/navigation gates would be needed before an Aptos target.

## NOAA historical seabed descriptions across all sectors

The official [NOAA/NOS seabed-description service](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/web_mercator/nos_seabed_dynamic/MapServer/0) was checked as a separate ground-truth lead. A complete, OBJECTID-ordered two-page query returned **3,591** original point records inside the California request envelope; the [coordinate-free sector receipt](../dist/data/noaa-seabed-samples-sector-review.json) assigns them to 14 of 19 browse bands. Crescent City–Humboldt, Del Norte, South Big Sur–San Simeon, Big Sur and Monterey–Point Sur had zero points in this service response. Usable source date years range from **1968 to 2016**; one record has an implausible **2080** value, which the pipeline counts and excludes from the range. Only 18 descriptions statewide contain an uncurated rock/boulder/cobble/bedrock word, and many nature-of-surface codes are absent or malformed. The [NOAA metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ngdc.mgg.geology%3AG10163) describes a mixed historical compilation, not a regular survey of every rock. Sparse points and keyword counts cannot delineate a fishing pile, show current fish, establish 200-foot depth or clear MPAs and rules. The original coordinate-bearing pages remain unpublished under `var/review`; the monthly substrate workflow rechecks their hashes and count, uploads only a coordinate-free review receipt and fails closed before any public aggregate changes.

The repeatable [original-cell cross-check](../scripts/review_noaa_seabed_native_overlap.py) compares those **18 uncurated rock-word points** with all 127 already audited fine regular NOAA BAG files, reading actual cells rather than counting only survey boxes. The [coordinate-free sector result](../dist/data/noaa-seabed-samples-native-overlap-review.json) finds 25 file-envelope intersections across 20 files but only **one distinct sample** on a measured, 25–200 ft MLLW cell with product uncertainty at most 1 m and the 2 m planning margin outside a 100 m MPA review buffer. That older Bodega–Point Reyes sample appears in two resolutions of the same later NOAA survey; it is **not two sites**. It is approximately **1,100 m** from the nearest already reviewed Bodega original hard-class outline, rather than inside one. Thus this sparse source cannot presently validate a fishing polygon. The monthly job checks for changes in the raw NOAA pages; after any approved source change, rerun this cross-check with the hash-pinned native BAG cache and fresh CDFW MPA geometry. Neither the static point nor a nearby bathymetry cell establishes today's fish, chart hazards, approach or legal take.

## Historical statewide reef and fish observations

CDFW's [CRFS combined RCGL block release](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/california-recreational-fisheries-survey-catch-per-unit-angler-for-rockfish-cabezon-greenl) supplies a directly recreational evidence stream for the whole California coast. The [complete source audit](../dist/data/cdfw-crfs-rcgl-sector-review.json) checked all **4,471** public one-minute block records against the July 2025 service schema and edition. Of 4,423 blocks assigned to the 19 approximate coast browse sectors, **2,667** had a positive combined rockfish/cabezon/greenling/lingcod catch-per-bottomfish-angler value in the 2021–2024 columns, **693** had zero, and **1,063** had the source's `-9999` unavailable marker. The remaining 48 source blocks fell outside these latitude bands. The coastal guide exposes only broad, attributed counts in its collapsed sector source details; no original block polygons, point marks, scores or exports use this dataset. CDFW reports interview locations, distributes catch across multiple blocks named on one trip, combines four target groups, includes fishing modes beyond private boats and requires three or more trips over the full 2004–2024 period. The `Samples` field is for that entire period, not the 2021–2024 denominator, so these block counts are **not** a comparable catch rate, species-specific habitat validation, current bite signal or legal fishing permission. Some service geometries are clipped relative to their nominal one-minute boxes. The [candidate record](../catalog/candidates/cdfw-crfs-rcgl-blocks.json) retains rights and precision limitations; the [monthly source workflow](../.github/workflows/monthly-fish-survey.yml) rechecks every page as an unpublished artifact and fails if CDFW changes the reviewed source edition.

The [NOAA DisMAP West Coast annual trawl source](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/69743) now has a separate [2023–2025 broad-sector audit](../dist/data/noaa-dismap-groundfish-review.json) and a [reviewed access candidate](../catalog/candidates/noaa-dismap-west-coast-groundfish.json). The direct NOAA ArcGIS layer returned **20,881** selected lingcod, Pacific halibut and named rockfish taxon records inside the California bounding box. The audit checks six count-matched, OBJECTID-ordered pages; source schema, service edit time and page hashes are retained. It groups distinct sampled hauls and positive/zero catch-per-unit-effort records by survey year, approximate browse sector and target. This is more recent independent biological *survey* context than the 2006–2019 diver archive, but it does not measure fishing success. NOAA's standard bottom trawl samples **trawlable shelf and slope**, missing many rocky and shallow inshore grounds; the source itself is derived from the NWFSC survey, so it is not a second independent trawl model. Only **eight distinct selected hauls statewide** in this three-year subset were at or under 200 ft, and a positive catch of a target at greater depth cannot validate a 200 ft recreational mark. No source catch coordinates, fish-hotspot pins, ratings or GPX waypoints are published. The [monthly fish-survey workflow](../.github/workflows/monthly-fish-survey.yml) fetches a fresh audit as an unpublished artifact and fails if the service introduces a new survey year, changes its required schema or returns incomplete pages; promotion to the public summary requires review.


The [NOAA IOOS / Reef Check California sampling-event archive](https://ipt-obis.gbif.us/resource?r=rcca_transects&v=1.5) is a useful independent biological source. Its CC0 Darwin Core release contains 19,375 dated scuba transects, 724,871 occurrence records (including explicit selected-taxon absences) and 255,300 environmental/size measurements from 2006–2019. The [pinned-source audit](../dist/data/reefcheck-statewide-observation-review.json) assigned 19,349 California events to 16 of the 19 approximate browse latitude bands; 26 events are outside those bands. The records include actual rockfish and lingcod sightings, substrate point-contact descriptions, relief and transect depth. They are **selected shallow reef/kelp sites**, generally around 5–18 m, with **250 m declared coordinate uncertainty on every event** and intentional MPA/reference-site sampling. Thus they can improve historical, shallow-habitat and species-method evidence, but cannot identify a small boat-fishing pile, calibrate offshore bite probabilities, establish a current fish location, or clear MPA access.

`catalog/reefcheck-observations.json` pins the original version, publisher, rights and SHA-256. `scripts/audit_reefcheck_observations.py` verifies all archive tables and emits only broad-sector summaries, without site coordinates. The monthly review retrieves the publisher's **latest** archive and fails if its bytes or table counts differ from the reviewed version; a changed source requires human review before republication. Its output stays unpublished until that review. This data remains outside target rankings, maps and GPX exports. Any future use for site-level modeling must separately handle repeated transects, survey selection, MPAs, species identification and coordinate error.
