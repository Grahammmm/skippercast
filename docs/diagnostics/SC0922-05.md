# SC0922-05: first-run brief timeouts on `e2e (2)` laptop

Scope: `e2e/v2-first-run-brief.spec.ts` cases `morro → boat → useful brief` and `duplicate profile activation saves and navigates once without waiting for warm-up`. Synthetic data only; GitHub run logs and the `e2e-results-2` trace artifact were read, nothing else was fetched.

## What the CI traces show

Run 41869630893 (job `e2e (2)`, GitHub-hosted runner, 4 Playwright workers, laptop project), traces `retain-on-failure`. Both attempts of the Boat case and the first attempt of the duplicate case ended with `Test timeout of 60000ms exceeded` in the **test body** (the trace's last action is a content assertion, not teardown); the CDP detach errors come after the expiry.

| Boat case, attempt 1 | Duration |
| --- | --- |
| Visit 1: `goto` to the visible verdict word | 11.7 s (budget 30 s, passed) |
| Visit 1: the five content/absence checks after it | about 11 s |
| Visit 2 (returning): to verdict word, then its checks | 9.8 s, then about 7 s |
| Reload: `page.reload` alone | 5.3 s |
| Reload: verdict word, then checks | 11.7 s, then the budget ran out mid-check |

Every phase passed its own 30 s funnel budget. The test failed because three phases, each allowed 30 s, share one 60 s limit, and about half of each phase was spent on locator round trips *after* the word was visible: single content checks took 1.2 to 4.6 s each.

Duplicate case: the verdict word was visible at about 8 to 11 s (inside the budget); then `expect(.app-verdict).not.toContainText(...)` took **33.8 s** for a 15 s expect timeout, the next dialog check 11.7 s, and the test expired during the following check. A page that cannot answer a locator query for 34 s is a main thread that is not yielding.

The same checks locally take 10 to 25 ms (probe and traces below). A passing CI sample (main, 41878505100) finished these cases in 22.2 s and 8.1 s in total, so the failing runs are about 3 times slower end to end, but their individual locator round trips are two orders of magnitude slower.

## What the page does after the verdict

The verdict is on the `/map` document, which starts the whole map straight afterwards (MapLibre worker and canvas, depth contours, ocean feed, the aerial base, protected-area query, glyphs). A probe on this branch (laptop, one worker, brief reached, then 3 s watched) recorded:

| CPU throttle | Long tasks in 3 s | Locator round trip (`not.toContainText`) | rAF gap max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 (as the spec leaves it) | 8 tasks, 53 to 248 ms, 956 ms total | 11 to 22 ms | 255 ms |
| 1 | 1 task, 153 ms | 3 to 4 ms | 154 ms |

The spec set `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` to 4 before `goto` and never lifted it, so the map's startup, a software-GL map on a shared 4-vCPU runner running four such pages at once, was multiplied four times into every locator round trip that followed the measured moment. That is the part of the wait the test controls.

## Disposition

Cause the test controls: the mobile CPU throttle outlives the thing it measures. The 30 s funnel budget is measured from the user's first request to the visible verdict word. Everything after that is a content assertion that has no time budget.

Change (test only): `verdict` still measures the budget under the 4x CPU throttle and the network throttle, exactly as before. As soon as the budget assertion has passed it lifts the CPU throttle (`cpu(cdp, 1)`), and the returning visit and the reload re-apply the throttle before their own timers start. All assertions, the 30000 ms budget, the 60000 ms test limit, the network throttle, the worker count and the retry count are unchanged. `test.step` names now separate "within the funnel budget (throttled)" from "content and absences (unthrottled)" in any future trace.

Regression: the Boat case and every cell assert the CPU rates actually sent over CDP, `[4, 1]` for a visit and `[4, 1, 4, 1, 4, 1]` for first visit, return and reload. Without the lift the list is `[4]` and the assertion fails.

After the change, with trace on, four workers pinned to four cores, the post-verdict content checks measure 5 to 66 ms each (before: 11 to 25 ms locally and 1.2 to 34 s in CI).

## What is not proven

- The 1.2 to 34 s round trips on the runner are not reproducible on this host (24 cores, faster than a hosted 4-vCPU runner): locally the same checks take milliseconds with or without the throttle. The runner's absolute slowdown (funnel word at 10 to 12 s against 4.5 to 7 s here) is runner load the test cannot control.
- The fix removes the 4x multiplier from the part of the timeline the test controls; it cannot be shown deterministic against an arbitrarily loaded runner. If `e2e (2)` still expires after this change, the next evidence to take is a Chromium performance trace on a runner, not a longer timeout.
