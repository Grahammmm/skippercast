# Southern California species and habitat evidence

Reviewed **September 22, 2026** for U.S. recreational private-boat fishing between Point Conception (34°27′ N) and the international maritime boundary, including the Channel Islands. This dossier supports repeatable regional profiles; it is not a current catch report or authorization to enter every island area.

## What this package adds

The regional menu has 16 targets: lingcod/rockfish; California sheephead; ocean whitefish; kelp bass; barred sand bass; spotted sand bass; California halibut; white seabass; California yellowtail; Pacific bonito; California barracuda; Pacific bluefin; yellowfin; dorado; California spiny lobster; and albacore as a secondary offshore option. Lingcod and rockfish remain separate biological and regulatory records behind one fishing selector.

Dungeness and salmon remain reference records for backward compatibility, but are not promoted regional targets. An unusual catch or an open legal season does not establish a useful local fishery. Spiny lobster is not a renamed crab: it has its own habitat, season, measurement, reporting and lawful methods. Spotted sand bass belongs to the bay/estuary workflow; offshore island rock does not become bay-bass habitat merely because it is within the same region.

## Species evidence and mapping decisions

Operational suggestions in the app are interpretations that need local validation. A mapped habitat class can be reasonably supported while current occupancy, legal-sized abundance and catch probability remain unknown.

The source links are primary agency documents or, for the bass telemetry summary, the research institution. Historical fishery reports support general ecology, not current rules or a present abundance trend. The CDFW life-history depth bands are broad habitat descriptions. They are not optimal fishing depths, a survey's coverage or permission to override a user depth setting. The kelp-bass report's shallow recreational depth context is explicitly identified as such.

## Channel Islands distinctions

[NPS describes the islands' kelp ecosystems](https://www.nps.gov/chis/learn/nature/kelp-forests.htm) in the meeting of different water masses. Preserve island name, side/exposure and observation date. San Miguel/Santa Rosa, the Santa Barbara Channel, Catalina and the southern outer islands must not share an assumed water temperature, current or reef-fish composition.

Maintain separate geometry for rock, connected sediment, observed canopy and pelagic search sectors. Preserve holes and boundaries. Canopy loss does not erase underlying rock, and a historic artificial-reef position does not provide current boulder dimensions. Bottom imagery should show the native measurements and resolution; never draw imagined boulders as though surveyed.

Spotted sand bass profiles are restricted to the mainland bay workflow. Whitefish, kelp bass, sheephead and lobster can be relevant island targets only where the specific habitat and lawful access support them. Mobile yellowtail, seabass and offshore fish need dated school/forage evidence; an island's name alone does not support a hotspot. AIS lingering, when available, remains evidence of vessel behavior, not identified catch or bottom structure.

The whole protected-area geometry must be screened, including state and federal extensions. Per the user's preference, SkipperCast withholds targets inside **all** mapped MPAs. That display policy is more conservative than law in some conservation areas. For example, [Anacapa](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Anacapa-Island) permits certain lobster/pelagic take in its conservation areas but has no-take reserves plus special net/trap and seasonal entry restrictions. “Pelagic finfish” is a legal defined list, not every fish that swims above bottom. White seabass exceptions can be method-specific elsewhere; [Blue Cavern](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Blue-Cavern) illustrates why species and method must both be checked. An MPA feed alone is not military, harbor or anchorage clearance.

## Reviewed legal changes and pitfalls

The cards use the [current CDFW Southern summary](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Fishing-Map/Southern), [general ocean rules](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Sport-Fishing/General-Ocean-Fishing-Regs), [invertebrate rules](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Sport-Fishing/Invertebrate-Fishing-Regs), [lobster page](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Invertebrates/Lobster) and the [2026 regulation booklet](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=239985). The PDF actually downloaded on this review date identifies itself as **last updated July 17, 2026**; an older search-index date was not used as its issue time. The Southern page identifies its update as September 1, 2026.

The structured cards contain size, bag, season, method and official source IDs for every target. Important interactions include:

- Bass share a five-fish total, with **at most four barred sand bass** in the current rule. Older species assessments that say only “five” are not the legal source.
- RCG, lingcod, sheephead and whitefish have separate species/group limits but still contribute to the general 20-finfish total. Ten RCG plus ten whitefish fills that total. Bluefin and regional albacore have explicit additional-limit exceptions; yellowfin does not.
- Sheephead's year-round diver/shore permission is not the Parker's boat rod-and-reel season. The legal cards retain the March–December boat season.
- Lobster is **hoop net or hand collection only**, with a report card even for children/assisting participants. The scheduled 2026 opener is **October 2 at 6 p.m. Pacific**, not midnight. The registry includes `start_at` so the app can distinguish that hour. The listed March 17, 2027 closing date is context; the reviewed registry still expires December 31, 2026.
- One hoop operator may use five hoops; multiple operators may use ten per vessel. Hoops need required marking and inspection at intervals no longer than two hours. There is a 24-hour pre-opener hoop prohibition south of Point Arguello.
- Anacapa's special closure prohibits nets/traps shallower than 20 ft. The northern West Anacapa pelican area has an additional January–October entry closure. A normal lobster opening does not remove these restrictions.
- Crab traps and loop traps are only authorized north of Point Arguello. The inherited Southern Dungeness trap/snare methods were removed instead of carrying Central Coast gear guidance into this region.
- The seasonal rockfish/lingcod RCA follows legally defined coordinates. A 300-ft sounder depth is not equivalent to crossing the legal 50-fathom line. Fish already aboard can retain closed-area and one-line/two-hook restrictions when switching targets.

**Recorded source conflict:** the Southern summary lists white seabass alternate length as 20 in; §28.35 in the retrieved booklet says 20½ in. Both agree on **28 in total length**, which is what the main card advises. The discrepancy is explicit; neither source was silently treated as agreeing with the other.

## Source authenticity, failures and repeatable maintenance

1. Keep species identity and habitat claims in `catalog/species.json`. Each claim has a primary source, evidence type, transfer limitation and confidence. Existing Central Coast species records remain intact.
2. Put regional life-stage, habitat, method, timing, depth context and evidence gaps in `catalog/ecology/california-southern.json`. A new region reuses biology where applicable and supplies its own habitat/access evidence; it does not inherit Southern coordinates or temperatures.
3. Put jurisdiction watches in `jurisdictions/california-southern.json` and reviewed summaries in `dist/regions/southern-california/regulations.json`. The existing daily collector now has explicit lobster and Anacapa watches, and the already-supported booklet job is also required by each legal card.
4. A successful page fetch does not approve a law. All new/expanded legal source fingerprints remain **null** until the actual normalized pipeline content has been compared and approved. `review_required` includes all sources. The manual research timestamp does not replace the last automated `checked_at` or invent a successful live check.
5. Keep survey date, resolution, depth qualification and geometry provenance with map data. Mobile-school observations need age, species, method, position precision and negative search effort. Distinguish missing observations from absence of fish.
6. Revisit ecology when new assessments or local observations materially change it; keep historical claims labeled. Check regulations daily and immediately before a trip, with in-season and exact method/position review. A changed or unavailable source must not produce an unqualified open indicator.
7. Validate all regional target IDs against ecology, shared species and legal cards. Confirm methods, all source IDs, positive/negative habitat cases and protected-area intersections before publication. Keep catch probability null until a local model is validated against held-out effort, including unsuccessful trips.

Some Marine Species Portal `/true` and `/the-species` endpoints failed during research. Their unread content was not used; readable CDFW PDFs and agency life-history pages supplied the relevant evidence. The NOAA Pacific mahimahi page had an intermittent timeout; the accessible NOAA-hosted 2024 HMS habitat appendix provides the dorado habitat reference instead. The MPA “Southern-California” network URL redirected to the general MPA overview, so the canonical overview is watched and the actual Anacapa rule page is a separate dependency.

The large CDFW booklet exceeded the web reader's PDF size limit. A verified-HTTPS download was parsed locally, with its byte hash and issue date retained under the ignored research directory. That raw-file hash is deliberately not used as a normalized web-watch approval. No screenshots, AIS positions, exact habitat coordinates, catch probabilities or current-stock estimates were invented in this research package.

## Review results and remaining evidence gaps

The final pass checked species names and life stages; mainland-bay versus island habitat; pelagic fish depth versus seabed depth; lobster methods and opening hour; finfish aggregates; seasonal boat-method differences; official links; and the documented white-seabass conflict. Corrected inherited Diablo Canyon text was removed from this Southern jurisdiction. New records do not assert calibrated bite scores or exact fishing spots.

The next data that can materially improve specificity are native-resolution bathymetry/backscatter, classified substrate and dated canopy; island-side observations of identified legal-sized fish; current forage/school reports with positional accuracy; and effort-normalized results including empty drifts/hoop lifts. A finer map should come from these measurements, not from adding unsupported points. Offshore reports must distinguish actual fish sightings from dock totals, commercial effort and charter vessel tracks.
