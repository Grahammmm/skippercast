# Monterey–Point Conception: closing the 300 ft evidence gaps

The [NOAA/NOS historic seabed-description service](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/web_mercator/nos_seabed_dynamic/MapServer/0) is now screened as a possible *independent physical-sample* source. The [repeatable gap audit](../dist/data/nos-seabed-central-gap-audit.json) queried all 250 returned records in a fixed central-coast envelope and measured distances to the 17 Monterey original-depth research outlines, 181 Point Buchon private 100 m research blocks and 60 Estero blocks. No sample lies inside any of those research geometries. None is within 500 m of a Monterey outline or Point Buchon block; their nearest reported samples are 3.7 km away (a 1985 record explicitly saying no sample was obtained) and 1.2 km away (1994 mud), respectively. Four 1994 samples are within 100 m of Estero blocks, but their descriptions are sand or mud, not rock, and the closest is still 29.8 m outside. These historic points have no verified horizontal error bound for a small pile. They therefore **do not validate substrate or fish at a target**; the audit is a useful negative control that prevents this attractive free data source from silently being promoted. Re-run `scripts/audit_nos_seabed_samples.py` after source updates, reviewing any new records and their survey precision before changing a qualification state.

The [Point Conception original 4 m patch stress test](../dist/data/point-conception-original-4m-patch-robustness.json) now checks *every retained native cell*, rather than assuming the two research components have uniform depth. H11952's 8 m-inset class-3 component is 4,736 m² and spans 69.408–73.473 m MLLW; the highest BAG product uncertainty is 0.617 m, and the greatest depth plus that cell's uncertainty and a separate 2 m planning allowance is 76.090 m, 15.350 m under the 300 ft limit. H11953's 3,616 m² component spans 61.046–62.320 m, with a 0.381 m maximum supplied uncertainty and 26.754 m minimum allowance-adjusted clearance. Its shallowest cell *minus* supplied uncertainty is 60.679 m, so the entire patch is **not confirmed deeper than 200 ft**, even though it lies safely under 300 ft in this screen. The interpreted hard-class geometry is also fragile: at a 12 m inward erosion H11953 has **no** component of at least 2,500 m²; at 16 m neither survey does. Those insets measure sensitivity, **not** positional error. [H11952's descriptive report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H10001-H12000/H11952/DR/H11952.pdf) documents GPSTide-to-MLLW processing, crossline QC and some GPS-position RMS spikes, but it does not bound the horizontal registration between this BAG and USGS class map at either patch. USGS class 3 can share acoustic lineage with NOAA, and the nearest original camera to the H11953 component is 145.6 m away. Thus the depth margin is promising as a *research* screen, while the independent substrate, position, biology, full route and trip-specific security gates remain open. The monthly substrate workflow now rechecks the exact full-patch statistics and holds changes for review.

The [machine-readable plan](../dist/data/central-300ft-qualification-plan.json) now gives every track an acceptance test and refresh trigger. For depth, request the original CUBE/BASE or BAG *uncertainty array* and processing report, not a chart screenshot. For habitat, seek video, grab or independently classified backscatter with negatives and a measured registration error, not another hillshade of the same DEM. The available [CDFW ROV program](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Management/monitoring/ROV) explains why its observations can support a depth-band habitat study; its published summary does not by itself place fish on a proposed waypoint.

Compare each feature against held-out open-water observations and future consented trips, retaining misses and no-catch effort. A proposed feature must improve out-of-sector prediction over a simple depth-plus-substrate baseline before it changes a rank.
