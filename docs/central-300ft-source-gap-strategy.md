# Monterey–Point Conception: closing the 300 ft evidence gaps

Updated September 26, 2026. This is the working release gate for the ten qualification steps in [the biological evidence plan](central-biological-evidence-and-gaps.md). A 1–3 **mapped habitat fit** rank is available for 132 earlier Morro–Avila candidates within the original 200 ft source screen. No additional 200–300 ft fishing target is qualified. Source acquisition may enlarge the research layer without enlarging the fishing layer.

The [machine-readable 300 ft qualification plan](../dist/data/central-300ft-qualification-plan.json) now joins the source queue, coverage ledger, original Point Buchon pair and independent Estero overlap. It gives each of the six survey sectors a reviewed lead, next acquisition, limiting evidence and the same ten release tracks below. The monthly Central source job rebuilds this plan byte-for-byte and holds changed evidence for review. Each of the six mandatory release gates remains false for each deeper patch; the plan cannot output a rank, waypoint or chartplotter export. This is an operational work queue, not a claim that all ten scientific and release tasks are finished.

The [2016 CDFW/MARE original-ROV acquisition lead](../catalog/candidates/cdfw-mare-ciap-2016-central-original-rov.json) is now linked to the five Monterey–Point Conception sectors it may cover. [MARE's survey account](https://mareresearch.org/?p=1798) reports 97.4 km post-processed across 33 sites and says the raw/processed Access database, cleaned ROV tracks, one-second sensor records, fish and substrate annotations, surveyed area, and GIS lines were delivered to CDFW. A [CeNCOOS data-stream plan](https://www.cencoos.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Appendix-F1.5-Coastal-Impact-Assistance-Program-CIAP-ROV-Survey-State-of-California-Data-Stream-Plan.pdf) says CDFW's `.mdb` was converted to CSV and made public through its [portal](https://data.caloos.org/#module-metadata/234576d4-347b-4c69-a986-1792c3023c8e/80b77bf3-273b-4fad-b660-22e3226d0547), WFS and downloads. The [live WFS audit](../dist/data/ciap-2016-central-rov-public-service-audit.json) confirms **236,960** timecoded Cruise E video-position features and **49,076** features in a separate, aggregated fish-display layer. These are not row-level fish observations joined to positions. The report-wide totals do not establish how many unprotected, 200–300 ft observations lie on any candidate. The next acquisition is the original or downloadable fish/substrate and effort tables, plus cleaned track quality, position gaps, zero-detection segments and taxon. Exact reuse terms and candidate-cell overlap also need review. A report map, a video point or a heat-map fish count cannot close the biological or substrate gates. The qualification builder hashes both the candidate and WFS audit and stops if either is relabeled as spot evidence without review.

### Search strategy for the remaining gaps

1. Query [NOAA NCEI's original NOS survey archive](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/nos-hydrographic-survey) by a narrow polygon and survey identifier, then open the actual BAG elevation, uncertainty and metadata arrays. A file name, catalog polygon or chart sounding only identifies an acquisition lead. The existing H11951–H11953 deep-band refutations and the measured W00614 cells near Pigeon Point show why cell-level screens must precede a spot claim. The newly pinned [W00614–USGS original-cell overlap](../dist/data/w00614-pigeon-original-character-overlap.json) found **zero valid USGS class pixels** at all 141,331 screened deeper NOAA cells, even though the two source bounding boxes overlap. The monthly source job repeats that refutation; a different independent substrate source is required there. [W00614's descriptive report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/W00001-W02000/W00614/DR/W00614_DR.pdf) lists unprocessed backscatter. It may help construct a future class model, but backscatter without independent groundtruth does not establish rock or fish.

   The new [H13152/W00479 original MLLW receipt](../dist/data/central-deep-original-300-refutation.json) hashes both complete BAG files and screens only refinements referenced by active supergrids, excluding unused trailing array padding. H13152's shallowest valid refinement is **341.034 m** and W00479's is **98.890 m**; neither has a measured cell at or shallower than 300 ft (91.44 m). This removes these *specific files* from the 200–300 ft acquisition leads in the sectors their catalog footprints cross. It does not imply adjacent Point Buchon, San Simeon or Estero habitat is absent. The monthly job redownloads and checks their original bytes and receipt before rebuilding the queue.

   A separate [Point Buchon hard-cell envelope check](../dist/data/point-buchon-noaa-catalog-envelope-gap.json) took the bounding envelope of **804,337** nominal 200–300 ft hard/rugose cells in the hash-pinned original USGS pair and queried NOAA's NOS BAG survey catalog specifically there. The fresh query returned **W00479 alone**; its original depth audit above shows no measured cells within 300 ft. This closes the *current NOS catalog query* as a near-term acquisition path for those cells. It is not an exhaustive survey inventory or a cellwise USGS/NOAA overlap. The next request is to USGS/CSUMB for the Point Buchon raster's output datum, total propagated uncertainty and processing records, while checking other archives or unpublished surveys. The monthly fish-survey job reruns the bounded query and holds a changed answer for review.

   The [original USGS video-catalog audit](../dist/data/w00614-usgs-video-observation-gap.json) adds an independent negative check for W00614. It reads all **13** linked original USGS observation archives (34,664 cataloged point records, including a potentially duplicative aggregate interpretation layer) and the same hash-pinned BAG's **141,331** qualifying cell centers. None of the video points lies on or within 100 m of the complete qualifying-cell envelope; the nearest archive's point is about **2.7 km** from that rectangle. Thus these available observations cannot ground-truth W00614's measured patch, even though the coastwide catalog contains rockfish and lingcod records elsewhere. This does not mean the patch lacks rock or fish. Search a different survey with actual video/grabs there, or collect new groundtruth before using W00614 backscatter to classify it. The monthly job will stop if the USGS catalog, sources or overlap changes.

   A more targeted [structured acquisition lead](../catalog/candidates/tnc-mlml-pigeon-video-lander.json), still not a spatial match, is [TNC's report to the Pacific Fishery Management Council](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2020/01/f5c_sup_pubcom4_apr2016bb.pdf/): a video lander observed canary and yelloweye rockfish at *Pigeon Point Reef*. This is a named reef, not a georeferenced drop table, and yelloweye retention is a separate regulatory issue. The [MLML/TNC stereo-video study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2024.1368083/full) confirms that its survey method recorded drop locations and species counts, but it has not been shown to contain the same 2016 drops; its published analysis groups drops into locations as wide as 50 km and does not supply the positions needed to compare with W00614. Request original bottom-position estimates (not just ship GPS), positional uncertainty, depth, habitat observations, date, taxon, no-fish drops, camera effort, MPA/reference role and reuse rights. First test their *actual* footprints against the 141,331 measured cells; a nearby reef name cannot retire the independent-substrate or biological gate. The NOAA 2018 ROV cruise described in the [West Coast Deep-Sea Coral Initiative report](https://spo.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/TMOHC12.pdf) worked from Bodega Bay to Año Nuevo, so it is not evidence of a W00614 match near Pigeon Point.

   The latter NOAA cruise can now be checked directly rather than dismissed from a broad cruise description. [NOAA's SH-18-09 dashboard](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/waf/dsc-data/dashboards/NOAA_GFNMS_CBNMS_SH-18-09.html) publishes 8,612 georeferenced coral/sponge occurrences, including Pigeon Point records. The [original ERDDAP-point audit](../dist/data/w00614-noaa-sh1809-observation-gap.json) hash-pins the selected CSV and compares all points with the W00614 eligible-cell envelope: 414 Pigeon-named occurrence records at 296 distinct record positions on August 3, 2018; 170 have listed depths of 200–300 ft. The closest published occurrence is about **514 m west of the rectangle**, beyond the source's stated 20 m location-accuracy field. None lies inside or within 100 m of the eligible-cell envelope. Because the database contains only coral/sponge *occurrences*, no fish counts or zero-observation effort, it cannot close the fish, independent-substrate or sampling gates. The monthly Central source job reruns the query and holds source/order/overlap changes for review. A full SH-18-09 ROV transect/video archive may contain useful nearby negative or bottom observations; obtain that separately and test its actual positions before changing any patch status.
   The [USGS Cal DIG I release](https://cmgds.marine.usgs.gov/data-releases/datarelease/10.5066-P9QQZ27U/) adds 2026 habitat polygons and original ROV annotation tables off south-central California. The [hash-pinned original-point audit](../dist/data/cal-dig-i-original-rov-300ft-gap.json) reads **44,429 biotic** and **8,332 substrate** rows: neither has a positive-depth observation in **200–300 ft** (60.96–91.44 m). The shallowest nonnegative records are 370.9 m and 371.0 m, respectively. The substrate table also has 691 negative depth entries, excluded as invalid rather than interpreted as shallow data. The [2026 CMECS metadata](https://cmgds.marine.usgs.gov/catalog/pcmsc/DataReleases/CMGDS_DR_tool/DR_P9QQZ27U/Cal_DIG_I_v2_CMECS_Metadata.html) states that substrate accuracy diminishes away from video transects, is unquantified and is a geologic interpretation; its vertical accuracy is undefined. Consequently the original ROV evidence cannot ground-truth a 300 ft patch, and a derived polygon is not a substitute for a matching shallow observation. This says nothing about fish or rock absence. The monthly job rechecks both complete original tables and holds a new shallow record for spatial review.
2. Use [NOAA BlueTopo contributor and coverage attributes](https://www.nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/data/bluetopo_specs.html) to discover upstream surveys. Its elevation may be measured or interpolated; an interpolated value never closes the native-depth gate. Hash-pin the original upstream product and retain native resolution, survey dates, datum, valid mask and supplied uncertainty.

   The [Point Buchon original-cell BlueTopo overlap](../dist/data/point-buchon-bluetopo-hard-cell-overlap.json) checks all eight current 4 m NOAA tiles across the original USGS hard/rugose research cells, with the official scheme, raster and contributor-table hashes pinned. All **804,337** nominal USGS centers fall on finite compiled BlueTopo pixels, but only **16** centers have a measured-survey contributor code (H05748 or H05832, dated 1934). The other 804,321 centers trace to interpolated historical data or generalization. This is a nominal center overlay across horizontal reference frames, not registered rock-scale depth validation. The current BlueTopo compilation cannot resolve Point Buchon's missing chart-datum and uncertainty gate, despite its complete-looking image.
3. Search [USGS CSMP original bathymetry and character products](https://cmgds.marine.usgs.gov/) and CSUMB survey packages for the same patch. Request or obtain the processing report, horizontal frame and epoch, total propagated uncertainty/CUBE surface, video-supervised class accuracy and redistribution terms. Do not turn terrain-derived roughness into an independent rock observation. The Point Buchon original metadata does not declare a vertical datum, so its nominal deep cell count remains source-datum research. The [pinned datum-provenance lead](../dist/data/point-buchon-datum-provenance-leads.json) records a [PG&E appendix](https://www.pge.com/assets/pge/docs/about/pge-systems/Ch2.GEO.DCPP.TR.12.01_R1_AppA.pdf) whose table labels a related CSMP multibeam survey NAVD88; that table does **not** establish that the later USGS GeoTIFF has the same processing lineage or output datum. [CSUMB's legacy library](http://seafloor.otterlabs.org/SFMLwebDATA_c.htm#BUCHON) lists separate 2007 Point Buchon reference and MPA grids, but the old archive endpoint timed out in this review. Obtain a custodian-confirmed crosswalk and original survey grids rather than assigning the PG&E datum to USGS cells by resemblance.

   Big Sur South Block 02's tempting 100% “accuracy” statistic is now [method-audited](../dist/data/bss02-accuracy-provenance.json): 100 random points compare a visual reading of the bathymetric shaded-relief image with a rugosity classification from that same bathymetry. It measures internal agreement, not independently observed rock. The original archive is hash-pinned and its method is checked in the monthly source workflow; a method change fails for review. Search separately for georeferenced video, bottom grabs, or a genuinely independent substrate survey on the same measured cells, including their negative observations and position error.

   An exact [USGS camera-to-Block 02 grid audit](../dist/data/bss02-video-grid-overlap.json) now tests the original 2012 cruise C0212SC against the hash-pinned 2011 native raster **valid mask**. Its rectangle encloses 46 camera records, including 39 interpreted-bottom windows, 11 rock/boulder windows, one rockfish-positive window and one lingcod-positive window. **None** is on a populated depth pixel or within 100 m of one. The source reports camera horizontal position as highly variable, on the order of 10 m; 100 m is a broad proximity screen, not a verified uncertainty bound. Thus these authentic nearby observations cannot ground-truth Block 02's measured cells or increase a spot rank. The monthly job reruns the spatial join and holds any changed result for review.

   The adjacent original [Block 03 video/grid audit](../dist/data/bss03-video-grid-overlap.json) finds a genuine, narrower evidence overlap: **46** 2012 camera windows nominally land on populated 2011 depth cells, all at **67.39–72.47 m** below the source NAVD88 datum (about 221–238 ft, not chart-qualified MLLW). Among those windows, **11** describe rock or boulder, one codes rockfish and one codes lingcod. Only **three** rock/boulder camera positions land on a derived rough-class pixel; **10 of 11** lie within 10 m of one and all 11 within 25 m. These windows belong to **one** nonempty camera line, so they do not represent 11 independent surveys. The source says camera positions vary on the order of 10 m against a 2 m raster: this is independent *nearby habitat evidence*, not an exact pixelwise confusion matrix or a current fishery-success estimate. It is the better Big Sur acquisition lead, but datum conversion with product uncertainty, rights, current MPA/chart/route screening, and biological scope still prevent a rank or export. The monthly source job verifies both original hashes and the join.

   The [private-footprint VDatum diagnostic](../dist/data/bss03-footprint-vdatum-diagnostic.json) samples NOAA's NAVD88 Geoid09→MLLW service at four corners and the center of each of the three 100 m research blocks. The original raster identifies `EPSG:26910` but does not identify its NAD83 realization or epoch, so requesting `NAD83_2011` is an explicit *diagnostic assumption*. Neither these 15 points nor the VDatum error represent a per-cell conversion or the original bathymetry's total error. The [official NCEI Block 03 catalog](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/ships/r_v_harold_heath/BSS_Block03_mb.html) links a 42,090,312,342-byte [CARIS project archive](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/ships/harold_heath/BSS_Block03/multibeam/data/version2/ancillary/BSSBlock03_caris.tar.gz). One line-processing log records “Compute TPU,” with uncertainty sourced from vessel settings and zero-valued tide uncertainty inputs; a later TPE binary member is listed. This does **not** prove a decoded CUBE/TPU surface, a defensible total error, or that the project projection was the final exported grid frame. Request a targeted processing/uncertainty and frame-crosswalk extract from the custodian before any 300 ft qualification. The monthly job rechecks this bounded prefix without downloading 42 GB or publishing block coordinates.

   A second [bounded original-byte audit](../dist/data/bss03-caris-original-tpe-member-lead.json) reads the first **64 MiB** of that official 42 GB archive and extracts one *complete* **37,668,488-byte** `HDCS` TPE member for a single line; the following TAR header proves the member was not truncated. Crucially, its path dates the line **October 4, 2011**, while the released Block 03 grid metadata list **November and December 2011** surveys. This member is **not shown to contribute to the grid**. It also does not decode depth/position TPU, establish error confidence or tide/datum contribution, or identify accepted soundings. Locate the contributing lines and obtain a documented export and source-to-grid crosswalk before using any value as an upper depth bound. The monthly job repeats the bounded read and holds changed source bytes for review.

   The separate 16,727-byte [original CARIS vessel-configuration archive](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/ships/harold_heath/BSS_Block03/multibeam/data/version2/ancillary/BSS_vesselconfig_caris.tar.gz) is now audited [member by member](../dist/data/bss03-original-vessel-tpu-inputs.json). The monthly job hashes and rechecks the small original archive; deeper targets remain held.

The monthly job refetches official sources and fails on a changed spatial result.

## Ten release tracks and their gates

The [machine-readable plan](../dist/data/central-300ft-qualification-plan.json) now gives every track an acceptance test and refresh trigger. The next source search is ordered by **which failed gate it can close**, not the number of datasets found. For depth, request the original CUBE/BASE or BAG *uncertainty array* and processing report, not a chart screenshot. For habitat, seek video, grab or independently classified backscatter with negatives and a measured registration error, not another hillshade of the same DEM. For fish, seek georeferenced open-reference transects with zero detections and swept area, then opt-in fishing trips with zero-catch effort. The available [CDFW ROV program](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Management/monitoring/ROV) explains why its observations can support a depth-band habitat study; its published summary does not by itself place fish on a proposed waypoint.

The resulting 1–3 **habitat-fit** decision will be made at the resolution of the weakest qualifying source. Record surveyed relief, contiguous independently confirmed hard-bottom area, edge length, depth band, fish-observation distance and positional support as separate features; do not count correlated cells or video frames as independent samples. Compare each feature against held-out open-water observations and future consented trips, retaining misses and no-catch effort. A proposed feature must improve out-of-sector prediction over a simple depth-plus-substrate baseline before it changes a rank. Publish both the rank and the limiting evidence; abstain when chart datum, registration, legal access or sample support is unresolved. A rank is not a catch probability or live fish detection.

1. **Cell-level coverage.** The current source queue, depth screens and original raster receipts identify sectors with real pixels, not catalog footprints. Next, build a common sector/cell matrix keyed by source hash, survey year, native resolution, observed/derived status, and missing reason. Pass only if every proposed patch has populated original cells.
2. **Depth and uncertainty.** Existing NOAA BAGs expose MLLW and measured uncertainty where available. The [2012 Estero Bay report](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2013/1225/) provides both a CORS96/NAVD88 source with 9,593,612 nominal 200–300 ft cells and a separate WGS84(G1150) ellipsoid-height source. The latter offers a direct VDatum path, as shown above. The separate within-cell sounding spread is **not** a total product-uncertainty bound, especially over rough bottom. The report says CARIS estimated sounding TPU, so request the original TPU/BASE surface. The [older frame-availability receipt](../dist/data/estero-2012-vdatum-frame-review.json) shows that WGS84_G1150 *paired with NAVD88* was rejected by the API; that does not contradict the newly accepted WGS84_G1150 *ellipsoid-height* input. Do not silently relabel CORS96 as NAD83(2011), substitute the CORS96 raster for the WGS84 source, or treat a point offset as a cellwise MLLW/error surface. Bound the entire candidate footprint plus a reviewed horizontal margin against 300 ft.
3. **Independent habitat.** Pair depth cells with source-classified seabed and its confusion/accuracy record. The new [Point Estero original-pair audit](../dist/data/point-estero-original-paired-200-300ft-review.json) has 1,176,749 nominal 200–300 ft hard/rugose cells within the same 2008 survey. More usefully, the [independent 2012-depth/2008-class overlap](../dist/data/estero-independent-2012-depth-2008-character-overlap.json) has 119 hard/rugose nominal cells in 200–250 ft and 24,091 in 250–300 ft. These are **research counts**, not individual rocks or lawful target footprints; the independent survey match has unresolved horizontal registration and no MLLW depth. The new Point Buchon original pair is another deeper local example. Class 2 is hard/flat, class 3 hard/rugose. Require overlap and a buffer that reflects registration error. Terrain roughness alone remains a model feature, not observed rock.
4. **Biological observations.** The CCFRP shallow catch/effort and published ROV deep open-reference summaries now run from pinned public releases. The [CDFW/MARÉ 2016 Soquel Canyon–Point Buchon report](https://mareresearch.org/?p=1798) is a targeted acquisition lead: it describes one-second cleaned ROV positions, survey-line shapefiles, sonar-measured swath, substrate classes and fish annotations at named sites including Big Creek, Piedras Blancas, Morro Bay and Point Buchon. CDFW's [program page](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Management/monitoring/ROV) says sites typically span 180–300 ft, but the public report's site maps do not provide a cellwise original table or position-error bound for our patches. Request the original georeferenced CDFW/MARE one-second observation and video/position audit trail, including zero-observation transects, fish taxon, swath, depth, uncertainty, protected/reference role and redistribution terms. Join actual bottom positions to eligible cells before changing a rank. No protected-site observation becomes a fishing coordinate.

   A fuller [OPC DataONE ROV fish-count release](https://opc.dataone.org/view/urn:uuid:7353f779-b722-4064-8074-3e9c651ed38e) is now [audited from its original CC BY 4.0 CSV](../dist/data/central-dataone-rov-300ft-spot-precision.json). It has 1,146,633 species rows statewide, including 47,225 Central Coast rows in 200–300 ft, which collapse to **370 location/date/depth/habitat units**, of which 157 are labeled Reference, 198 MPA and 15 N/A. At reference sites the broad historical signal is real: 23 of 28 Big Creek units and 4 of 10 Point Buchon units have a positive lingcod entry; 27 of 28 and 7 of 10 respectively have a positive rockfish-group entry. Those are sampled units, not independent trips, catch probabilities or a comparison normalized by swath. The release spans 2007–2020. The published latitude/longitude has at most **two decimal places**, about kilometer-scale rounding; 265 positive count rows are fractional despite the metadata labeling Count as individuals. The table does not contain the original one-second positions, individual video observations, swath or positional-error trail. Its underlying records may overlap earlier processed ROV releases; independence is unverified, so it is not counted as a second survey. It cannot place a boat on a pile. The monthly fish-survey job redownloads the original object, checks the full hash and holds any finer coordinates or changed semantics for review. Use this source as regional historical context only until original precise, effort-linked records are obtained.
5. **Sampling scope.** Keep CCFRP cell-trip effort and ROV 10 m subunits separate. A species-specific map layer should carry observation years, detection method and sample size; neither catch rate nor ROV count is a current bite forecast. A rank change requires a reviewed biological-to-habitat linkage at the *same* supported spatial scale.
6. **Current fishery evidence.** CRFS/RecFIN blocks, dockside reports and dated charter reports can update broad activity and seasonality. Obtain opt-in private trips with effort, gear, depth, position accuracy, unsuccessful drifts and safe sharing rules. Only consented, aggregated data can influence public maps; no individual boat track is published.
7. **Law and safe access.** Refresh CDFW MPA geometry, federal closures, in-season species rules, security areas and NOAA ENC hazards for the fishing date. Intersect full target, drift, approach and return footprints with uncertainty buffers. A clean center point alone never clears an area. Store source effective date and review receipt.
8. **Evidence ladder.** Keep research outline, depth/habitat-qualified, legal/chart-screened, and field-verified states separate in data and UI. Show the limiting gate on every card. The Point Buchon paired audit remains research-only despite ~804,000 nominal hard/rugose cells in the depth band.
9. **Calibration.** Collect future trip outcomes before fitting spot effectiveness. Pre-register a species baseline, hold out later trips and different coastline sections, include zero-catch effort, and report skill and abstentions. Do not train and validate on the same CCFRP/ROV survey locations.
10. **Promotion and refresh.** Monthly source jobs fail on changed bytes or schema and upload review artifacts. A human-reviewed release promotes a patch only after the preceding gates pass; rebuild atlas ranks and exports from the promoted manifest, run regression and geospatial checks, and retain previous releases for rollback. A source update cannot silently publish a new fishing mark.

The highest-value external request now is a Point Buchon/Big Sur survey processing package with output vertical datum, per-cell or survey-level **upper** uncertainty, horizontal registration and independent seabed classification. The second is the CDFW/MARE original ROV observation trail. Until those arrive, prioritizing a *research* patch is useful; giving it a precise chartplotter waypoint or a fishing-quality rank would overstate the evidence.
