# Monterey–Point Conception: closing the 300 ft evidence gaps

Updated September 26, 2026. This is the working release gate for the ten qualification steps in [the biological evidence plan](central-biological-evidence-and-gaps.md). A 1–3 **mapped habitat fit** rank is available for 132 earlier Morro–Avila candidates within the original 200 ft source screen. No additional 200–300 ft fishing target is qualified. Source acquisition may enlarge the research layer without enlarging the fishing layer.

The [machine-readable 300 ft qualification plan](../dist/data/central-300ft-qualification-plan.json) now joins the source queue, coverage ledger, original Point Buchon pair and independent Estero overlap. It gives each of the six survey sectors a reviewed lead, next acquisition, limiting evidence and the same ten release tracks below. The monthly Central source job rebuilds this plan byte-for-byte and holds changed evidence for review. Each of the six mandatory release gates remains false for each deeper patch; the plan cannot output a rank, waypoint or chartplotter export. This is an operational work queue, not a claim that all ten scientific and release tasks are finished.

### Search strategy for the remaining gaps

1. Query [NOAA NCEI's original NOS survey archive](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/nos-hydrographic-survey) by a narrow polygon and survey identifier, then open the actual BAG elevation, uncertainty and metadata arrays. A file name, catalog polygon or chart sounding only identifies an acquisition lead. The existing H11951–H11953 deep-band refutations and the measured W00614 cells near Pigeon Point show why cell-level screens must precede a spot claim. The newly pinned [W00614–USGS original-cell overlap](../dist/data/w00614-pigeon-original-character-overlap.json) found **zero valid USGS class pixels** at all 141,331 screened deeper NOAA cells, even though the two source bounding boxes overlap. The monthly source job repeats that refutation; a different independent substrate source is required there. [W00614's descriptive report](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/W00001-W02000/W00614/DR/W00614_DR.pdf) lists unprocessed backscatter. It may help construct a future class model, but backscatter without independent groundtruth does not establish rock or fish.
2. Use [NOAA BlueTopo contributor and coverage attributes](https://www.nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/data/bluetopo_specs.html) to discover upstream surveys. Its elevation may be measured or interpolated; an interpolated value never closes the native-depth gate. Hash-pin the original upstream product and retain native resolution, survey dates, datum, valid mask and supplied uncertainty.
3. Search [USGS CSMP original bathymetry and character products](https://cmgds.marine.usgs.gov/) and CSUMB survey packages for the same patch. Request or obtain the processing report, horizontal frame and epoch, total propagated uncertainty/CUBE surface, video-supervised class accuracy and redistribution terms. Do not turn terrain-derived roughness into an independent rock observation. The Point Buchon original metadata does not declare a vertical datum, so its nominal deep cell count remains source-datum research.
4. Request the original georeferenced [CDFW/MARE ROV sampling trail](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/MPAs/Management/monitoring/ROV) with zero detections, effort, fish taxon, observation year, transect width and positional error. Separate open reference sites from protected MPAs, and use fish counts only at their supported footprint. Dated charter and consenting angler trips can later estimate broad effectiveness if they include unsuccessful effort; public AIS tracks alone cannot establish fishing or catches.
5. For each candidate patch, convert a reviewed depth surface to MLLW with propagated error, compare it to independent substrate and observations, then screen its entire drift/approach/return footprint against current CDFW, federal, security and chart data. Preserve a source date, fingerprint and failed-access record for each check. Only after all gates pass should a reviewer set the 1–3 habitat-fit rank, publish the target manifest and run export regressions. Field outcomes should calibrate later revisions using held-out dates and coastline sections, including zero-catch drifts.

The most useful next imports are: **(a)** the Point Buchon total depth uncertainty and declared output datum or another MLLW survey over its 200–300 ft hard cells, **(b)** the 2012 Estero CARIS TPU plus its CORS96-to-current-frame and NAVD88-to-MLLW surfaces, **(c)** a *different* independent rock classification at W00614's measured Pigeon Point cells, and **(d)** Big Sur/CSUMB original uncertainty, rights and substrate linked cell by cell. The plan prioritizes these by what they can resolve; it does not treat more sources or older observations as automatic increases in spot accuracy.

Search queries should be by **bounding polygon and survey ID**, not by town name or a chart label. The queue now joins the independent Estero 2012 depth and 2008 character receipts by pinned source hash. Its 24,210 nominal hard/rugose overlap cells are a source-acquisition priority only; it explicitly records the datum, registration, uncertainty, legal, chart and biological hold. The monthly audit regenerates the receipts but cannot promote that lead into a fishing mark.

The [Estero research-block screen](../dist/data/estero-2012-depth-class-closure-block-review.json) aggregates those cells into 60 **100 m review blocks** (7 in the nominal 200–250 ft band and 53 in 250–300 ft). A fresh bounded CDFW query returned five named MPAs, NOAA returned ten Groundfish Exclusion Area polygons, and NOAA ENC Direct completed 18 bounded obstruction/awash-rock/wreck queries. None intersected the full blocks plus a 100 m margin. Zero ENC danger features returned is not a complete chart or safe-transit finding. The exact block geometry remains an unpublished research artifact under `var/review/`; no ranked point or export is created. The monthly job reruns all three official source checks and fails if reviewed geometry or screening counts change.

The original [2010 CSUMB SCC Block12 and Block13 grids](../dist/data/csumb-scc-2010-estero-original-block-coverage.json) provide an independent depth cross-check in **four of the seven** shallower Estero blocks. Their 2 m grids contain no measured cells in any of the 53 deeper research blocks, so they cannot validate the 250–300 ft lead. In the nominal shallower overlap, nearest-cell comparisons to the 2012 survey have median 2010-minus-2012 differences of −0.230 m and −0.164 m, with 95th-percentile absolute differences of 0.494 m and 0.523 m. These statistics mix survey years, Geoid09 versus Geoid12, registration and possible bottom change; they are **not** a per-cell depth-uncertainty bound. The SCC habitat layers are terrain derived from the same grids, not independent rock or fish observations. Monthly original-byte checks pin both archives and keep this coverage result research-only.

## Ten release tracks and their gates

1. **Cell-level coverage.** The current source queue, depth screens and original raster receipts identify sectors with real pixels, not catalog footprints. Next, build a common sector/cell matrix keyed by source hash, survey year, native resolution, observed/derived status, and missing reason. Pass only if every proposed patch has populated original cells.
2. **Depth and uncertainty.** Existing NOAA BAGs expose MLLW and measured uncertainty where available. The [2012 Estero Bay report](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2013/1225/) now provides a stronger original NAVD88 source: 9,593,612 source-datum cells in the nominal 200–300 ft band, with separate within-cell sounding spread. That spread is **not** a total product-uncertainty bound, especially over rough bottom. The report says CARIS estimated sounding TPU, so request the original TPU/BASE surface. Transform the source's NAD83(CORS96) frame at its documented epoch to the frame required by VDatum; the [frame-availability receipt](../dist/data/estero-2012-vdatum-frame-review.json) shows that direct API attempts with WGS84_G1150 or NAD83_NSRS2007 plus NAVD88 returned HTTP 200 JSON `errorCode: 412` (“Source Horizontal Frame should be NAD83_2011”), while a NAD83_2011 point request returned an offset. Do not silently relabel the survey as NAD83_2011. Build a reviewed NAVD88→MLLW *surface* with propagated horizontal and vertical error, then test the entire polygon plus a 2 m planning margin against 300 ft. No scalar point offset substitutes for this.
3. **Independent habitat.** Pair depth cells with source-classified seabed and its confusion/accuracy record. The new [Point Estero original-pair audit](../dist/data/point-estero-original-paired-200-300ft-review.json) has 1,176,749 nominal 200–300 ft hard/rugose cells within the same 2008 survey. More usefully, the [independent 2012-depth/2008-class overlap](../dist/data/estero-independent-2012-depth-2008-character-overlap.json) has 119 hard/rugose nominal cells in 200–250 ft and 24,091 in 250–300 ft. These are **research counts**, not individual rocks or lawful target footprints; the independent survey match has unresolved horizontal registration and no MLLW depth. The new Point Buchon original pair is another deeper local example. Class 2 is hard/flat, class 3 hard/rugose. Require overlap and a buffer that reflects registration error. Terrain roughness alone remains a model feature, not observed rock.
4. **Biological observations.** The CCFRP shallow catch/effort and published ROV deep open-reference summaries now run from pinned public releases. Request the original georeferenced CDFW/MARE one-second observation and video/position audit trail, including zero-observation transects, fish taxon, swath, depth, uncertainty and reference/MPA role. No protected-site observation becomes a fishing coordinate.
5. **Sampling scope.** Keep CCFRP cell-trip effort and ROV 10 m subunits separate. A species-specific map layer should carry observation years, detection method and sample size; neither catch rate nor ROV count is a current bite forecast. A rank change requires a reviewed biological-to-habitat linkage at the *same* supported spatial scale.
6. **Current fishery evidence.** CRFS/RecFIN blocks, dockside reports and dated charter reports can update broad activity and seasonality. Obtain opt-in private trips with effort, gear, depth, position accuracy, unsuccessful drifts and safe sharing rules. Only consented, aggregated data can influence public maps; no individual boat track is published.
7. **Law and safe access.** Refresh CDFW MPA geometry, federal closures, in-season species rules, security areas and NOAA ENC hazards for the fishing date. Intersect full target, drift, approach and return footprints with uncertainty buffers. A clean center point alone never clears an area. Store source effective date and review receipt.
8. **Evidence ladder.** Keep research outline, depth/habitat-qualified, legal/chart-screened, and field-verified states separate in data and UI. Show the limiting gate on every card. The Point Buchon paired audit remains research-only despite ~804,000 nominal hard/rugose cells in the depth band.
9. **Calibration.** Collect future trip outcomes before fitting spot effectiveness. Pre-register a species baseline, hold out later trips and different coastline sections, include zero-catch effort, and report skill and abstentions. Do not train and validate on the same CCFRP/ROV survey locations.
10. **Promotion and refresh.** Monthly source jobs fail on changed bytes or schema and upload review artifacts. A human-reviewed release promotes a patch only after the preceding gates pass; rebuild atlas ranks and exports from the promoted manifest, run regression and geospatial checks, and retain previous releases for rollback. A source update cannot silently publish a new fishing mark.

The highest-value external request now is a Point Buchon/Big Sur survey processing package with output vertical datum, per-cell or survey-level **upper** uncertainty, horizontal registration and independent seabed classification. The second is the CDFW/MARE original ROV observation trail. Until those arrive, prioritizing a *research* patch is useful; giving it a precise chartplotter waypoint or a fishing-quality rank would overstate the evidence.
