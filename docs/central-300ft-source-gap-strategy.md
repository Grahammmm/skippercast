## Ten release tracks and their gates

1. **Cell-level coverage.** The current source queue, depth screens and original raster receipts identify sectors with real pixels, not catalog footprints. Next, build a common sector/cell matrix keyed by source hash, survey year, native resolution, observed/derived status, and missing reason. Pass only if every proposed patch has populated original cells.
2. **Depth and uncertainty.** Existing NOAA BAGs expose MLLW and measured uncertainty where available. USGS/CSUMB research patches remain held. Get each survey's processing report and datum definition from the producer; create a reviewed NAVD88→MLLW *surface* with propagated horizontal and vertical error, then test the entire polygon plus a 2 m planning margin against 300 ft. No scalar point offset substitutes for this.
3. **Independent habitat.** Pair depth cells with source-classified seabed and its confusion/accuracy record. The new Point Buchon original pair is the first deeper local example; class 2 is hard/flat, class 3 hard/rugose. Require overlap and a buffer that reflects registration error. Terrain roughness alone remains a model feature, not observed rock.
4. **Biological observations.** The CCFRP shallow catch/effort and published ROV deep open-reference summaries now run from pinned public releases. Request the original georeferenced CDFW/MARE one-second observation and video/position audit trail, including zero-observation transects, fish taxon, swath, depth, uncertainty and reference/MPA role. No protected-site observation becomes a fishing coordinate.
5. **Sampling scope.** Keep CCFRP cell-trip effort and ROV 10 m subunits separate. A species-specific map layer should carry observation years, detection method and sample size; neither catch rate nor ROV count is a current bite forecast. A rank change requires a reviewed biological-to-habitat linkage at the *same* supported spatial scale.
6. **Current fishery evidence.** CRFS/RecFIN blocks, dockside reports and dated charter reports can update broad activity and seasonality. Obtain opt-in private trips with effort, gear, depth, position accuracy, unsuccessful drifts and safe sharing rules. Only consented, aggregated data can influence public maps; no individual boat track is published.
7. **Law and safe access.** Refresh CDFW MPA geometry, federal closures, in-season species rules, security areas and NOAA ENC hazards for the fishing date. Intersect full target, drift, approach and return footprints with uncertainty buffers. A clean center point alone never clears an area. Store source effective date and review receipt.
8. **Evidence ladder.** Keep research outline, depth/habitat-qualified, legal/chart-screened, and field-verified states separate in data and UI. Show the limiting gate on every card. The Point Buchon paired audit remains research-only despite ~804,000 nominal hard/rugose cells in the depth band.
9. **Calibration.** Collect future trip outcomes before fitting spot effectiveness. Pre-register a species baseline, hold out later trips and different coastline sections, include zero-catch effort, and report skill and abstentions. Do not train and validate on the same CCFRP/ROV survey locations.
10. **Promotion and refresh.** Monthly source jobs fail on changed bytes or schema and upload review artifacts. A human-reviewed release promotes a patch only after the preceding gates pass; rebuild atlas ranks and exports from the promoted manifest, run regression and geospatial checks, and retain previous releases for rollback. A source update cannot silently publish a new fishing mark.

The highest-value external request now is a Point Buchon/Big Sur survey processing package with output vertical datum, per-cell or survey-level **upper** uncertainty, horizontal registration and independent seabed classification. The second is the CDFW/MARE original ROV observation trail. Until those arrive, prioritizing a *research* patch is useful; giving it a precise chartplotter waypoint or a fishing-quality rank would overstate the evidence.
