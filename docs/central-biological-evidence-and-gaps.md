# Monterey–Point Conception biological evidence and 300 ft gaps

Reviewed September 26, 2026. This is a **source-acquisition and validation plan**, not a new set of fishing coordinates. The 300 ft physical screen and legal holds remain in [the 300 ft review](central-coast-300ft-review.md).

## What can be measured now

The [CCFRP 2007–2024 release](https://www.ccfrp.org/publications-reports) has standardized hook-and-line catch, zero-catch observations and angler-hours in matched MPA and open reference rocky-reef cells. Its [DataONE package](https://opc.dataone.org/view/urn:uuid:2250743c-a813-42c1-aeaa-f8b142b77199) carries CC BY 4.0 rights. The method selects 500 × 500 m cells in water under 40 m; some individual records reach about 45.5 m. It therefore improves **historical, shallow, regional biological context**, not the 200–300 ft source gap. The [machine-readable open-reference summary](../dist/data/central-ccfrp-area-evidence.json) contains no cell coordinates and no MPA catch records. It was built from 366,630 source rows with source hashes pinned in [the acquisition manifest](../catalog/ccfrp-2024-source.json).

These are observed program catch rates under its particular timing, gear, survey cells and anglers; they are **not** expected charter or private-boat catch rates, not present-day bite forecasts, and not species abundance estimates for every reef in the named area. Zero-catch cells remain in the denominator. The broad rockfish group needs separate species treatment: a blue-rockfish observation cannot validate a vermilion-rockfish target.

The [CDFW 2014–2016 ROV report](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=159232&inline=) independently surveyed Central Coast hard, mixed and soft bottom with georeferenced fish observations. Its methods measure substrate, swath, depth and fish counts; the Central Coast cruise spans Monterey Bay to Point Buchon and includes sites at depths relevant to 200–300 ft. The report publishes cruise/area summaries, but SkipperCast has **not** obtained and audited the underlying one-second transect database. Published aggregate fish counts cannot be assigned to a specific open-water target or used as an effort-calibrated fishing recommendation. ROV coverage inside MPAs is research evidence only; never convert protected-site observations into fishable points.

A [CDFW/Ecotrust CPFV rockfish and lingcod ground layer](https://filelib.wildlife.ca.gov/public/BDB/GIS/BIOS/metadata/DS1091.html) describes captains' 2011 fishing-ground interviews from Pigeon Point to Point Conception. It is neither AIS history nor precise drift locations; values are area and revenue-weighted, confidentiality-smoothed, and thirteen-plus years old. Its metadata says **contact Ecotrust about using this data**. Keep it out of the public derivative and ratings until permission, currentness and spatial accuracy are reviewed. Public charter reports can provide dated activity context, but landing totals do not locate a fish.

## Acquisition order and promotion gates

1. **Complete a sector-by-sector 25–300 ft source matrix.** For each existing research polygon, record which original NOAA BAG, USGS/CSUMB bathymetry, independent substrate map and biological survey actually covers its cells. Reject catalog footprints, source filenames, compiled bathymetry and 500 m catch grids as proof of a 2 m rock. The [source queue](../dist/data/central-source-acquisition-queue.json) already separates reviewed fine cells from broad survey leads.
2. **Resolve depth and uncertainty before drawing a precise target.** Record original horizontal/vertical datums and epoch, source resolution, per-cell uncertainty and survey date. Convert NAVD88 surfaces to MLLW with a reviewed *surface* transform and propagated error, not a handful of VDatum point probes. A cell near the 300 ft cutoff must remain inside it after uncertainty and planning margin.
3. **Pair every bottom cell with independent habitat evidence.** Use source-classified rock, boulder and soft sediment where available; keep terrain-derived roughness explicitly modeled rather than observed rock. Check mismatched resolution and registration, gaps, outdated seabed and the survey class's measured accuracy. Do not rank a rough bathymetry pixel as a boulder field by inference alone.
4. **Acquire the CDFW/MARE ROV transect database or a documented public equivalent.** Require survey date, taxon, image or observation ID, depth, substrate, position uncertainty, area swept and MPA/reference role. Aggregate by comparable habitat/depth bins, with zero-observation transects retained. A report table or a transect inside an MPA cannot validate an adjacent fishable waypoint.
5. **Use CCFRP only within its sampled depth/gear scope.** The pinned [rebuild script](../scripts/build_ccfrp_area_evidence.py) downloads and hashes the official package, checks license and protocol, de-duplicates angler effort, excludes protected records and fails when the publisher lists a newer release. The [monthly job](../.github/workflows/monthly-fish-survey.yml) checks for source drift and uploads review-only results; new years or changed methods require human review before a new pin. Stratified species/season summaries need sufficient independent trips and out-of-sample evaluation before altering 1–3 spot ranks.
6. **Add current fishery-dependent data carefully.** CDFW CRFS/RecFIN blocks and dock reports inform broad season and effort, not exact reefs. Seek de-identified voluntary trip logs that retain zero-catch drifts, species, method, anglers, fishing minutes, bottom depth and approximate position accuracy; never silently treat missing reports as zero catch. Restrict sharing of individual tracks by default.
7. **Screen the *whole usable footprint* for legality and approach.** Refresh CDFW MPA and in-season groundfish boundaries, federal closures, Diablo/Vandenberg access and NOAA chart hazards. Test target polygons, drift line, approach and return—not only a center point. Depth/area rules depend on trip date, method and species. Keep law-source issue date and review receipt distinct from the map data date.
8. **Publish an evidence ladder.** Research outline → depth/habitat-qualified area → legally/chart-screened candidate → field-verified spot. Show the source age, raster resolution, uncertainty, biological support and missing gates on each card. A 1–3 habitat fit rank is an ordinal *mapped-habitat* judgment, never a catch probability or a promise that the fish are there.
9. **Calibrate using holdout trips.** For each species, compare simple habitat/season baselines with more complex rankings on future trips and spatially separate areas. Include unsuccessful trips, effort and gear; report discrimination, calibration, coverage and abstention. Do not promote a new predictor because it fits the same survey used to define it.
10. **Keep acquisition and publication separate.** Source checks and derived research receipts run on a schedule; a changed license, schema, datum, closure, uncertainty or source age fails closed. A reviewed release promotes only qualifying geometry and rankings, preserving prior evidence and an audit trail. All unqualified 200–300 ft polygons remain research-only and absent from chartplotter exports.

The highest-yield next external requests are the georeferenced CDFW/MARE ROV observations and a complete original bathymetry-plus-substrate pair for the unscreened sector patches. Until those arrive and the datum/route gates pass, the honest result for exact new 200–300 ft waypoints is **unverified**.
