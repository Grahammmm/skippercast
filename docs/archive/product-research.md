# SkipperCast: product research and feature candidates

Research date: **September 21, 2026**. Reviewed official product/help pages for **13 representative products**, plus government and Global Fishing Watch documentation. This is a feature and data review, not hands-on testing of paid subscriptions or validation of vendors' catch-success claims. No purchases, accounts or registrations were made.

The strongest direction is a compact fishing-planning companion: conditions and uncertainty at a glance, a useful legal habitat map, then private feedback from the user's own drifts. Existing chartplotter apps should remain easy export destinations. Rebuilding every navigation feature would delay the parts most useful on a 23-foot Parker.

## What SkipperCast already has

The pre-change repository documents a mobile Map/Conditions/Guide shell; combined lingcod/rockfish and other species selectors; reef terrain grades and geometry; connected sediment regions; static drift alignments; NOAA chart portrayal; GPX exports; seven-day wind/wave comparison; tides and live observations; dated regulatory cards; and charter **named-ground reports**, not verified charter AIS stops. It already distinguishes conditions, confidence and unknown bite potential. Sources: repository `docs/web-app.md`, `docs/roadmap.md`, and `docs/charter-grounds.md`, read September 21, 2026.

Those foundations do not yet provide an offline trip pack, private trip log, calibrated boat drift prediction, routed departure/return plan, validated species catch model, or verified local charter AIS heatmap. Several candidates below refine existing features instead of adding more controls.

## Product coverage and important limits

- **Navionics/Garmin Boating, iNavX, Aqua Map, C-MAP:** reviewed for charts, offline operation, data handoff, routes, instruments and weather overlays. Paid-chart presence is not permission to reuse their maps in SkipperCast.
- **Fishbrain and FishAngler:** reviewed for species filtering, private locations, catch logs and condition presentation. Their marketing descriptions are evidence that the features exist, not evidence that proprietary bite scores accurately predict Morro Bay catches. FishAngler's [FAQ](https://home.fishangler.com/faq/) describes a solunar-based forecast while newer product pages describe broader inputs; do not infer a fully disclosed, validated model from those pages.
- **FishTrack, RipCharts, Terrafin and ROFFS:** reviewed for offshore ocean imagery, saved maps, front interpretation and evidence presentation. Terrafin's root page exposed little readable content; its official iPad manual was readable, but has no clear current publication date, so present-day app-store/platform availability was not verified. ROFFS explicitly [distinguishes satellite imagery and cloud-free modeled imagery](https://roffs.com/faqs/); SkipperCast should do the same without repeating the vendor's accuracy claims.
- **Windy, PredictWind and Windfinder:** reviewed for dense conditions, comparison, personalization and trip motion. Source documentation does not establish local forecast skill. Multiple apps displaying NOAA GFS are one underlying forecast model, not independent agreement.
- **Global Fishing Watch:** relevant research/data product, not counted among the 13 fishing/weather apps. Its [dataset documentation](https://globalfishingwatch.org/dataset-and-code-fishing-effort/) calls the output *apparent fishing effort* and documents much poorer representation of small vessels. Do not imply that absent charter AIS means absent fishing. No local vessel history was obtained as part of this competitor-review subtask.

## Data work with the largest payoff

1. **Finish the legal and geometry layer first.** Version official closure geometries and screen both points and every proposed line/area. Link directly to the relevant current CDFW page; a cached polygon is not a regulatory update by itself.
2. **Build a small evidence table for every displayed forecast sample.** Keep provider/model, run metadata where available, valid time, retrieval time, grid coordinate, units, coverage and QC. Use this both for dense display and confidence, rather than maintaining conflicting UI-specific rules.
3. **Collect real user drift/effort before claiming bite prediction.** A 5-minute GPS test drift and private catch/no-catch log are practical calibration targets. Consider [NOAA/IOOS surface currents](https://ioos.noaa.gov/data/access-ioos-data/) only where time, geometry and quality are valid. [IOOS STPS](https://ioos.noaa.gov/models/short-term-predictive-system-stps/) describes 24-hour forecasts; regional population/coverage must still be checked before using it locally or implying seven-day availability.
4. **Treat AIS as its own research pipeline.** Historical receiving coverage and vessel identity are prerequisites. Publish only defensible clusters at source-supported precision, with separate charter/commercial labels, selected gear and date ranges. Do not narrow broad report polygons into fabricated GPS hotspots.
5. **Add offshore satellite fronts after the core workflows are reliable.** Archive acquisition/quality metadata and track persistence over several observations. A useful front is a moving search feature, not a permanent dot.

All external links above were accessed September 21, 2026. Feature availability can vary by region, platform and subscription. Priorities, implementation approaches and the proposed drift workflow are SkipperCast design judgments, not claims that competitors implement them identically.
