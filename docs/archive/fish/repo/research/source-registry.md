# SLO Fish Report source registry and pipeline specification

Reviewed October 1, 2026. Research uses the `skippercast-discover-data` skill, SkipperCast's read-only catalog and Morro Bay configuration, primary publisher documentation, and bounded HTTP requests. [Access receipts](access-probes.json) preserve requested URLs, response sizes and status. The adjacent response files are research receipts, not public page copy. A successful HTTP request establishes access at review time; it does not establish completeness, freshness or all publication rights.

The probe receipts include the exact tested high/low tide request, MUR regional subset, MPA count query and all CDIP metadata endpoints. The implemented live collector also verified `interval=6`: 1,200 six-minute rows spanning September 30–October 4, with 19 high/low events. It verified marine grids LOX/62,133 (north), LOX/68,119 (central), and LOX/73,110 (south), each with 168 populated hourly weather slots, and real NWS `updateTime` metadata. URL templates must be assembled with encoded query parameters.

## Visibility and bite-window gaps

No source reviewed here measures underwater visibility continuously at the local dive sites. Atmospheric visibility, water temperature, clear weather, chlorophyll and offshore waves cannot establish “15 feet visibility.” Begin with **visibility unconfirmed**, supplemented by dated, depth-specific diver observations. Record horizontal/vertical visibility definition, depth, site/position precision, observation time, observer and verification. Retain observations only as local evidence for a short stated period; never extrapolate across headlands.

Potential clarity predictors are antecedent rainfall/runoff, local wave energy and duration, swell exposure, substrate resuspension, wind, nearshore Kd490/remote-sensing reflectance and chlorophyll. These are a future calibrated model, not a current direct clarity measurement. NOAA's [OLCI ocean color](https://coastwatch.noaa.gov/cwn/products/ocean-color-near-real-time-olci-sentinel-3a-and-3b-regional-coverage.html) is a candidate for 300 m Kd490/reflectance: exact SLO cloud-free footprint, quality flags, latency and terms still require testing. Harbor T-pier chemistry from [CeNCOOS](https://www.cencoos.org/observations/what-we-observe/) is a candidate estuary observation; it does not cover open-coast dive sites.

Likewise, a favorable tide plus dawn is an **opportunity window**, not a demonstrated bite prediction. First estimate method-specific practical windows from daylight, access, tide-height constraints and conditions. Store each reason and species assumptions. Before estimating catch probability, gather trip-level effort, targets, zero catches, gear, depth and date/location under a defined sampling scheme. Separate calibration by species and fishing method; validate prospective accuracy and compare against a simple seasonal baseline. Charter counts alone do not meet this requirement.

## County-independent design

Use one provider adapter per source family and one county configuration per publication. A county sets locations/exposure, discovered NWS points, tide references, observation stations, CDIP nearshore points, species/method profiles, legal management areas, catch publishers, harbor notices and source freshness budgets. Keep provider response shapes out of report templates. A new county requires geographic and source checks, not copying Morro's stations.

Normalize records with `source_id`, `producer`, `delivery_service`, `kind` (observation/forecast/analysis/report/rule), `retrieved_at`, `observed_at` or `issued_at`, `valid_from/through`, native coordinates/footprint, sampled coordinates and distance, units, datum, quality flags, source version/hash, attribution and publication rights. Preserve original direction conventions: winds/waves usually **from**, current vectors **toward**. Forecast slots must carry input IDs and assumption version so a daily narrative can be rebuilt and corrected.

Recommended jobs: observations 15–30 min; alerts 5–10 min; NWS 30 min; CDIP 1 h; WCOFS hourly catalog checks but only new cycles downloaded; MUR daily with recent revision checks; catch counts evening/morning; legal pages daily with review queue. Publish a detailed today report plus 48–72 h conditions outlook. Later NWS weather remains useful as a lower-confidence trend, while currents cease at actual WCOFS horizon. Never fill a missing report ingredient with a fabricated number.

For Cloudflare, thin JSON/text/ASCII pulls can run in Workers cron with timeout, byte caps, jitter, ETags, exponential backoff and endpoint allowlists. Complex GRIB/NetCDF processing is better precomputed by an offline scheduled job or a container, then ingested into R2/D1; direct bounded OPeNDAP ASCII is a practical first choice for CDIP/WCOFS. Keep immutable raw receipt objects in R2 and indexed normalized/latest report records in D1. Atomically publish a report manifest only after required sources pass checks. Each source can degrade independently; retain its last valid record with visible age. Do not equate ingestion success with an approved report.

SkipperCast's native bathymetry/habitat exports should remain a separate versioned dependency, with coordinate/depth uncertainty and legal review retained. The source audit shows conditions are implementable now, but reliable exact fish abundance, bite probabilities, dive visibility and verified charter AIS activity remain separate evidence gaps.
