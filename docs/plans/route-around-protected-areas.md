## 1. Today

- `waterRoute` finds a water-only path on a grid. It snaps each end to a reachable water cell, searches neighbouring cells without cutting blocked corners, then straightens bends where the water checks permit. The distance adds the two end connectors and the segments between the bends (`web/route.ts:173–228`, `:255–290`).
- Its optional `allows(points)` hook checks cell centres, the connectors, every search move, and shortcuts during smoothing. The centre decisions are cached for that search only, separately from the shared shoreline cache (`web/route.ts:82–84`, `:129–137`, `:173–185`, `:200–219`, `:271–286`). The separate GPX planning tool already uses this hook and rechecks its full line. It excludes all screened polygons plus a planning margin; that is not the Boat brief and is not a transit rule (`web/protected-route.ts:29–55`).
- Where to Look starts the Boat list routes after paint, one per task; its call to `waterRoute` has no `allows` filter. The brief reads the same saved results and uses the selected listed place or the first listed place. A route that is pending or fails leaves a labelled straight-line distance; a computed water route can push a pick out of range (`web/brief/WhereToLook.tsx:73–91`, `:108–134`; `web/brief/Run.tsx:77–92`, `:165–185`; `web/ranking.ts:523–539`).
- `runRouteScreen` sends the timed line (harbor, snapped ends and bends, destination; or just the ends for a straight line) to the shared screen. `screenPlace` checks coverage, then looks for the nearest protected area; `distanceM` returns zero for touching, overlapping or contained geometry. The brief warns on a crossing or touch, or within the 70 m planning margin. A missing, stale, wrong-region, uncovered or failed screen is “protected areas not checked,” not clear. A checked line with no hit or near hit gets no note, and the note does not change the distance or time (`web/brief/run.ts:127–160`; `web/brief/Run.tsx:95–105`, `:179–185`; `dist/geo-screen.js:69`, `:161–174`, `:216–249`).

## 2. Which closures count

**A no-take area is not necessarily closed to boats passing through.** A reviewer must decide from the current rule text, not from a polygon, its name, its type or a fishing-target exclusion. For California MPAs use [California Code of Regulations, Title 14, § 632](https://wildlife.ca.gov/CCR-T14-MPAs), including its general and area-specific provisions. For federal areas use the applicable federal rule and any current effective orders or amendments; the [NOAA West Coast Groundfish Closed Areas page](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/sustainable-fisheries/west-coast-groundfish-closed-areas) is a starting point, not a transit verdict. The repo describes DS582 as display geometry, and the supplemental map explicitly defers to federal regulations (`docs/archive/fish/repo/research/spatial-review.md:17–21`; `reports/rules/RULES-CHECK-2026-11.md:42–52`; `dist/protected-areas.js:10–15`, `:36–40`).

**Proposed rule for the app:** Block only a current, reviewed transit prohibition that matches the exact area and boundary revision and applies to this vessel and both legs for the whole planning window. The review must identify the authority, citation, effective times, geometry match and any exceptions. A conditional rule the app cannot establish, a conflict, an expired or unmatched rule, or an area with no reviewed rule stays a warning, not a block. Keep all existing fishing-target and GPX gates unchanged. The 70 m warning margin is not a legal boundary; do not use it to turn a near miss into a transit prohibition (`dist/protected-areas.js:36–40`, `:59–61`, `:70–97`; `web/brief/run.ts:154–159`).

**UNVERIFIED:** No production polygon has been qualified here for transit blocking. The boundary screen reports geometry and freshness, not whether passage is prohibited. Leave production avoidance inactive unless separately admitted, already-reviewed rule inputs exist; the slices below use synthetic rule inputs only (`dist/protected-areas.js:23–32`, `:70–112`; `tests/test_run_route.mjs:552–556`).

## 3. Behaviour

The following is proposed, not current behaviour:

| Situation | What the brief should do |
| --- | --- |
| No applicable reviewed block intersects the existing line | Keep the current estimate and warning behaviour. Do not search for a detour or say passage is allowed. Warnings still apply to unreviewed areas (`web/brief/run.ts:142–159`). |
| An applicable reviewed block touches or crosses the existing line, including a labelled straight line | Check only the place already chosen by the brief, not every pick. After paint, try one water detour with `allows`: check each whole segment, not just its ends. Use just the reviewed blocking polygons for that search; apply the ordinary warnings to the full final line. Check the full harbor-to-place line again, including both connectors, against the same current boundary and rule inputs before using it (`web/route.ts:129–137`, `:173–185`, `:200–219`, `:263–286`; `web/protected-route.ts:39–54`). |
| Detour found and checked | Say “Planning distance by water around reviewed closures.” Recalculate run and fishing times from that distance. Keep the same destination and label the brief's distance separately from the list's; if the detour exceeds the user's range, say so and withhold times. Do not move the pick, reorder the list, or change GPX or fuel output in this work (`web/brief/run.ts:55–78`, `:96–115`; `web/brief/Run.tsx:77–92`, `:179–195`; `web/ranking.ts:523–539`). |
| No route found that avoids a known block | Say “No planning route found around the checked transit closures. Check current official rules and charts.” No departure, turn-back or fishing-time estimate on the blocked line or on a straight-line substitute. This includes a blocked endpoint or channel with no usable detour in the grid; it does not mean passage is illegal everywhere (`web/route.ts:173–188`, `:224–229`, `:290`; `web/brief/run.ts:96–115`). |
| Not finished or not checkable | Pending: “Checking for a planning detour.” Limit reached, missing shoreline or coverage, invalid geometry or a failed recheck: “Could not check a planning detour.” Do not restore the ordinary estimate if an applicable reviewed prohibition might affect the line but the check did not finish. With no reviewed prohibition applicable and the boundary screen unavailable, retain the ordinary estimate and “Route: protected areas not checked.” Discard old detours when region, pick, shoreline, boundary snapshot, rule set or its time validity changes; re-evaluate rather than displaying the previous distance. Keep existing no-window and no-speed messages (`web/route.ts:24–36`, `:269–290`; `web/brief/Run.tsx:95–105`, `:165–185`; `web/brief/run.ts:96–102`, `:142–159`). |

**Phone CPU budget — UNVERIFIED.** Proposed acceptance target: at most 100 ms p95 of added computation per selected run on a low-end supported phone, including data copy, geometry preparation, search, smoothing and final check; no added main-thread task of 50 ms or more. Start with one deferred job at a time in a dedicated browser worker, with a 40,000-node ceiling (the separate planning tool's starting value) and a 250 ms wall-clock cutoff from job launch that stops the worker and reports “could not check.” Run the existing-line intersection check there too when reviewed rules apply; until it answers, do not show times as if the check had passed. Bound input size and count geometry work too: the existing node cap does not cover preparation, snapping, or smoothing. Do not raise the current list or router caps. Before enabling, record cold and warm measurements on an offline phone fixture for a short detour, a complex boundary and a blocked channel; include cancellations and timeouts, not just successes. The existing water-only Node timing assertion is not a phone measurement (`web/route.ts:24–27`, `:173–222`, `:259–269`; `web/protected-route.ts:29–31`; `web/brief/WhereToLook.tsx:119–134`; `tests/test_route.mjs:157–166`).
