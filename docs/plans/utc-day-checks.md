## R2. What happens in the California evening

Morro Bay uses `America/Los_Angeles` (`regions/morro-bay/region.json:6`). UTC has already advanced from **16:00 PST until local midnight** (8 hours) or **17:00 PDT until local midnight** (7 hours), start included, midnight excluded. Reproduce the start times with `TZ=America/Los_Angeles date -d 2026-11-22T00:00:00Z '+%F %T %Z'` and `TZ=America/Los_Angeles date -d 2026-07-22T00:00:00Z '+%F %T %Z'`. These are clock differences, not estimates of visitor traffic or failure rates.

- **Chart:** If the file expires on the current Pacific date, a qualifying load in that window won't draw its contours. The legend reads “Depth contours withheld: their source review is past its one-year age limit.” A tab that already loaded the file does not drop the overlay just because the clock crossed midnight UTC: the assessment runs on load and the draw effect uses the retained result. A new overview/full read can change the result (`web/map/depth-contours.ts:118,217–219,410–456`; `web/app/Legend.tsx:389–406`).
- **Planner intake:** In the same window on the expiry date, a newly checked file leaves “Depth contours withheld…” with “Turn off depth contour tracks to export.” GPX Download and Share are disabled while that refusal applies. Off bypasses the contour check, not the other export checks; a region with no listed file does not show this expiry refusal (`web/planner.ts:152–161,273–284,353–358`; `web/app/Planner.tsx:351–363`).
- **Final export:** Even if the Planner got valid contours before the cutoff, a new export build in the same window can fail with “Depth contours cannot be exported: source review expired or undated.” The status can show at validation or on delivery; normal Notes also runs the builder. This is not a claim that an already-prepared file is reassembled every time (`dist/trip-export.js:62–73,136,161`; `web/planner.ts:282–284,539–551,697–737`).
- **Area reasons:** In that window, a bare date equal to UTC tomorrow is no longer considered future. If it otherwise qualifies and makes the top three, it can show with tomorrow's date or displace another reason. No effect for already eligible dates (`web/area-reasons.ts:22–25,33–41,62–79`; `web/brief/WhereToLook.tsx:241`).
- **Skill stamp:** A scorecard generated within that evening window is stamped one day ahead of the Pacific publication date. The stamp is tied to generation, not the viewing clock: it stays the same on later visits. No earlier stale cutoff and no different percentage from the formatting alone (`web/brief/skill.ts:39–61`).
- **Neighboring cases:** The tide request endpoints advance one GMT date in that window, but even the shifted start is midnight UTC at the start of the current Pacific date's UTC label (the prior Pacific afternoon), so the range still covers the entire current Pacific day. This is a request-range inference, not an observed NOAA response (`web/tide-stations.ts:87–94,150–152`; `dist/marine-data.js:264–280`). The regional controls in R1 have **zero hours** of early UTC rollover. For the unmounted helpers, the ENSO age boundary would be 7/8 hours before the corresponding Pacific midnight if the retrieval check passes; the fixed-offset advisory would end about one hour early on a PST `through` date, not 7/8 hours early; and the kelp quarter could advance 7/8 hours early only on the eve of a quarter boundary, not daily (`dist/coasts.js:43–62,83–89`; `web/map/kelp.ts:130–147`). None of these last three is a confirmed visitor symptom.

## R3. Contract or accident?

- **Explicit UTC contract:** `assessContours` documents UTC and says “past that date” (`web/map/depth-contours.ts:169–173`); `>` makes the expiry date inclusive (`:217`). The Chart and Planner honor that contract, despite the Planner's separate region day. The final export's independent comparison implements the same rule; it does not separately document a competing time zone (`dist/trip-export.js:59–73`). The producer stamps a date based on the oldest source review and says “withheld from”; it does not specify a time zone. Confirm what “through” should mean before changing runtime behavior (`src/skippercast/platform/depth_contours.py:560–568,602–625`).
- **Explicit GMT purpose, not expiration:** The two NOAA request builders send `gmt`; the station version explicitly calls for parity with the other builder and offline keying (`web/tide-stations.ts:87–94`; `dist/marine-data.js:264–280`; `dist/sw.js:137–146`). Forecast and alerts deliberately start from a regional date (`dist/forecast.js:47–64`; `web/alerts.ts:55–56,76–85`).
- **Looks incidental or needs confirmation:** Area evidence promises “not after `now`,” not a UTC date policy; the `Date.UTC` representation supplies that policy for bare dates (`web/area-reasons.ts:33–41,57–59`). Skill's UTC date is unlabeled display copy; `/skill` is labeled UTC, so the Brief choice may be inherited rather than an intended local date (`web/brief/skill.ts:39–43,58–61`; `dist/skill-page.js:47–50`). ENSO explicitly uses midnight UTC and the collector uses the same assumption, so it is existing policy in code, not a solo browser conversion (`dist/coasts.js:47–49`; `src/skippercast/pipeline/coastal_watch.py:133`). The advisory hardcodes `-07:00` and kelp deliberately reads the UTC quarter; neither is evidence for changing the contour contract (`dist/coasts.js:58–62`; `web/map/kelp.ts:130–139`).

## R4. Smallest consistent change — recommended first

**If the owner wants contours usable through the expiry date in the mapped region**, switch all three contour paths together to the region's civil day at the time of each check. Do not just pass the Planner's opening-day value: it was captured at setup; a loaded collection also goes through a separate final check. Reuse one region-day formatter/comparison for Chart, Planner intake and export, keeping `>` and the other fail-closed checks. Use the checked region's configured zone, not the visitor's device zone or the selected fishing date. Files to scope: `web/map/depth-contours.ts`, `web/planner.ts`, `dist/trip-export.js`, and the existing pure date helper if shared there (`web/trip-times.ts:42–46` is an example of region formatting). The Chart currently has the region ID, not a zone argument: if passing the zone from the Chart mount, also scope `web/app/MapStage.tsx:220`. Owner approval is needed for the documented UTC change and inclusive meaning; no producer or data migration is proposed (`web/map/depth-contours.ts:169–173,217,410–418`; `web/planner.ts:219,273–284,353–358,539–551`; `dist/trip-export.js:59–73`).

**Focused future tests:** Extend `tests/test_depth_contours.mjs:59–70` (expiry equality plus overview/full controller), `tests/test_planner_retry.mjs:138–175` (withheld message, off bypass, and correct day passed at a fresh load), and `tests/test_trip_export.mjs:280–309,374–401,438–452` (optional tracks, direct guard and already-loaded file). Use an otherwise-valid fixture and fake clock on both sides of UTC midnight **and** Pacific midnight in winter and summer. With the proposed contract, a contour expiring on the region date passes throughout that date, then fails at region midnight. Hold the device zone different from the region and vary the draft date; neither should move the cutoff. Keep invalid/held files refused and contours-off export behavior unchanged. These are proposed regressions, not tests executed by this report.

**If the owner wants to keep UTC:** keep all three clocks as they are. If clarification is needed, change only the withholding/refusal wording to name UTC (`web/map/depth-contours.ts:118`; `web/planner.ts:154`; `dist/trip-export.js:63–73`), with the same boundary tests locking in the current early-evening Pacific cutoff. Do not convert the NOAA range, forecast or alerts controls. Area evidence is a separate presentation decision: if it should not show tomorrow's bare-date reasons, scope `web/area-reasons.ts` and its Brief caller separately; compare only bare dates with the region day and keep full-instant ordering (`web/area-reasons.ts:33–41,67–74`; `web/brief/WhereToLook.tsx:241`). `web/brief/skill.ts` is listed for completeness; the sibling owns it and no work on it is proposed here.
