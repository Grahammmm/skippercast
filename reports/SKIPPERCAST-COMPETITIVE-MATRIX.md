# SkipperCast competitive matrix: features, map layers, user needs and what to build

Card COMPETITOR-MATRIX-1 (t_bf284d4a), opus-coder. Research date **2026-11-07**. SkipperCast baseline read at `main` 5fd63730e3 (2026-11-06). Machine-readable twin: [`catalog/competitor-matrix.json`](../catalog/competitor-matrix.json); every table below is generated from it.

## For G: one screen

SkipperCast already does things the 26 products below mostly do not document: it screens every suggested place, leg and export against protected areas, shows native 2 m survey bathymetry and hard-bottom classes with their survey and date, saves rules, MPA outlines and the brief in an offline pack, and says how old every layer is. Its clear gaps against what anglers already get, often free: it never shows **where you are** on the map, it has **no daily per-boat landing counts**, **no tidal current at the bar**, and alerts need a saved trip and an account. In the public reviews we counted, the most common complaint across apps was **paywalls on things users already had**, and the most common specific praise (tied with "everything in one app") was **"shows me where I can legally fish"**. Free California MPA maps are not a moat (FishLegal and CDFW both offer them); the moat is the integrated, dated, whole-plan answer.

**Build next, in order:**

1. **Live position on the Chart** with the protected-area reading for where you are, offline too (medium).
2. **Lead with the offline legal pack** we already have: offer it for every saved trip, show each rule's dates offline, check it is complete before you leave (small).
3. **Route around protected areas** and export the plan as a GPX route (medium).
4. **Daily landing counts per port and boat**, with filed time, once the owner settles the held report-site rights (medium; rights first).
5. **2 m bathymetry and hard-bottom detail beyond Morro Bay**, depth and survey at the cursor (large; some sources rights-held).
6. **Tidal current timing at the harbor entrance** (medium; data availability unverified).
7. **One freshness stamp and one update cadence per layer and rule** in the legend (small).
8. **Simple wind and swell alerts for a home port** without saving a whole trip; Windfinder charges for alerts and Windguru limits free ones (small).
9. **Measured forecast skill in the brief**, beside the model-disagreement line the brief already shows (small).
10. **One-tap catch or blank entry** from the map with position and conditions attached, private (small).

Do not build: a public catch map or social feed, a bite score, turn-by-turn navigation, passage routing, unsourced hot-spot pins, paywalls on data a user already has, or live charter-fleet AIS hotspots.

Things the feature matrix shows plainly:

- **MPA and closure maps are common, not rare.** FishLegal (free, offline), CDFW (free, online only), Navionics (per the California MPA outreach page) and SatFish ("where available") all document them. What no page read documents is screening a *whole plan* (every spot, leg and export) against them, which SkipperCast does. That absence is UNVERIFIED, not proven.
- **Offline is table stakes for anything used on the water.** Every chart app, SatFish, RipCharts Premium, FishTrack and FishLegal document it; most charge for it. SkipperCast's pack is free and includes rules and closures, but only when the user asks for it.
- **California regulations are thin outside FishLegal, CDFW and Fishbrain.** Fishing Points' rules cover only FL, TX, GA, NC and LA; FishAngler documents land access, not fishing rules; Fishbrain puts regulations in Pro.
- **Daily California landing counts now have a free app.** Fish City documents boat-by-boat counts including Morro Bay and Avila Beach with push alerts. This is new since the 2026-10-31 retro and is the closest direct competitor to SkipperCast's Reports view.
- **Live position is documented by FishLegal, the CDFW map, the chart apps, SatFish and RipCharts; SkipperCast has none on the map.** Its only geolocation use is choosing a nearby port ([web/ports.ts](../web/ports.ts) `locatePort`).

### Layer detail: resolution, update cadence, coverage, tier

Every layer cell with a cited source has three attributes. Each is either what the cited page states, **UNVERIFIED** (the public page cited does not state it; nothing was tested), or n/a (resolution for outline and point layers). Where a vendor states coverage only for the whole product, the cell says so ("UNVERIFIED per layer; product-wide coverage the vendor states: ...") rather than presenting it as a per-layer fact. A vendor's forecast *range* or *time step* (for example "5-day", "3-h steps") is labelled as such and is not an update cadence. Counted over competitor cells that document a layer, by render.py: resolution: stated 13, UNVERIFIED 73, n/a 14; cadence: stated 23, UNVERIFIED 77, n/a 0; coverage: stated 36, UNVERIFIED 64, n/a 0. Most cadences and resolutions are not published, which is itself a finding: a layer's age and grid are rarely shown to the user.

Reading the layer matrix:

- **Satellite ocean layers (SST, chlorophyll, true colour, altimetry) are the paid fishing-map core** (SatFish $129/yr, RipCharts $99-169/yr, FishTrack Premium, FishDope $199/yr). SkipperCast has free observed SST, temperature contrast, fronts with age and uncertainty, chlorophyll and HF-radar currents, so it is at or near parity on the "find the edge" layers; it lacks true colour and altimetry.
- **Bathymetry splits in two.** Chart apps sell fine contours (Navionics SonarChart HD "1 ft" contours, C-MAP HRB "1-ft level of detail", both paid; both are contour detail, not a stated grid resolution) without survey source or date on the pages read; satellite-map apps show shaded relief of unstated resolution. SkipperCast shows native 2 m USGS grids with survey year and datum, plus hard-bottom classes, but only around Morro Bay so far. NOAA BlueTopo is a free national compilation updated weekly to monthly.
- **Hard-bottom and habitat is SkipperCast's clearest unique layer.** The only other hardness layer found is Navionics sonar imagery "on select lakes".
- **Tidal currents and weather radar are the common layers SkipperCast lacks.**

### Ranked list

Effort: small = days of one builder; medium = one to three weeks; large = a month or more, or blocked on data rights. Rough, not a delivery promise.

1. **MUST-HAVE: Show the user's live position on the Chart, with the protected-area reading for that position, offline too.** Why: FishLegal (free) and the CDFW web app (free) both show position against MPA boundaries; SatFish, RipCharts, C-MAP Premium, iNavX and Navionics show GPS on their maps. SkipperCast only calls geolocation to choose a nearby port (web/ports.ts locatePort, dist/home-port.js). Legal-position praise was the second most frequent praise code in the review sample (5 mentions, 2 apps), and position freezes were a complaint in chart apps (3 mentions, 2 apps), so show fix age and accuracy. Effort: **medium**. Data: Device geolocation only; reuse the existing protected-area screen and 70 m planning margin; no new source.
2. **DIFFERENTIATOR: Lead with the offline legal pack that already exists: offer it for every saved trip, show each rule's effective and saved-at date offline, and check the pack is complete and current before departure.** Why: The pack already stores regulations, MPA boundaries, additional closures and the brief with charts, contours and ocean layers (dist/offline-core.js DAILY_PARTS), but it is saved only on request from the account menu. FishLegal's free tier is offline MPA maps and rules; the CDFW map needs internet; Navionics and C-MAP keep offline behind a subscription. Offline praise appeared in 3 apps (4 mentions) and offline download failures in 2 apps (3 mentions), so the completeness check matters. Effort: **small**. Data: Existing pack parts and their revision dates; no new source.
3. **DIFFERENTIATOR: Route around protected areas and export the planned route as a GPX route.** Why: Roadmap item 4 still lists it. Navionics, C-MAP, Aqua Map and Savvy Navvy document autorouting; none of the pages read documents routing that avoids MPAs or screens a whole plan (UNVERIFIED for them, not proven absent). SkipperCast already screens every point and leg, so this extends a real lead. Effort: **medium**. Data: MPA polygons, land-water screen, web/route.ts; GPX <rte> writer; planning-only wording kept.
4. **MUST-HAVE: Daily landing counts per port and boat, with filed time and 'location not stated'.** Why: Fish City (free, iPhone) documents boat-by-boat daily counts from California landings including Morro Bay and Avila Beach, with NOAA conditions and push alerts. SoCal Fish Reports does the same on dated URLs. SkipperCast's Reports view has dated catch cards but no per-boat daily count table. Effort: **medium**. Data: Landing count sources. Rights are HELD: the data-rights register marks TECK.net report sites 'needs permission' for paid use and the facts-only free use is an open owner question (Q3). Do not start before the owner decides.
5. **DIFFERENTIATOR: Extend 2 m shallow bathymetry and hard-bottom classes beyond Morro Bay, with depth and survey at the cursor.** Why: Navionics (SonarChart HD 1 ft) and C-MAP (HRB 1 ft, Premium) sell fine contours, but the pages read give no per-area survey date or source, and Navionics reviews in the sample complain of wrong or degraded depth (2 mentions). SkipperCast shows native 2 m USGS grids with survey year and datum, and hard-bottom classes, which no product page read documents for California. Effort: **large**. Data: USGS CSMP 2 m bathymetry and seafloor character, NOAA BlueTopo; CSUMB Cambria grids are rights-held; per-source licence review.
6. **MUST-HAVE: Tidal current timing at the harbor entrance.** Why: Navionics, iNavX, SatFish (3-6x/day), PredictWind Standard and Fishing Points document tidal currents; SkipperCast states its tides are 'not Morro Bay bar currents'. The bar is the limiting fact for small boats on this coast (2026-10-31 retro). Effort: **medium**. Data: NOAA CO-OPS current predictions or a validated model; whether a CO-OPS current station covers the Morro Bay entrance is UNVERIFIED.
7. **DIFFERENTIATOR: One freshness stamp and one published update cadence per layer and rule, visible in the legend.** Why: SatFish and RipCharts each publish two conflicting update rates (2026-10-31 retro). FishLegal reviews dispute rule currency (2 of 10 reviews). SkipperCast already has basis sentences and /status; making age visible on every layer turns that into an advantage users can see. Effort: **small**. Data: Existing feed metadata and rule revision dates.
8. **MUST-HAVE: Lightweight condition alerts for a home port (wind, swell) without saving a full trip.** Why: Spot alerts are common in weather apps, but mostly not free: Windfinder lists wind alerts under 'Windfinder Plus upgrade' as Plus only (https://www.windfinder.com/apps); Windguru gives free users 'Limited' e-mail forecast alerts and full alerts with PRO at EUR 25.90/yr (https://www.windguru.cz/help.php?sec=pro); Windy lists favourite-spot mobile or e-mail alerts in its general feature list, and they were not on the Premium list read, but no page states their tier, so a free Windy alert is inferred, not verified. SkipperCast alerts need a passkey account and a saved trip; a free, no-trip port alert would match the best of these, not merely catch up. Effort: **small**. Data: Existing forecast tiles and alert engine.
9. **DIFFERENTIATOR: Show measured forecast skill in the brief, beside the model-disagreement line it already has.** Why: The brief already renders model disagreement: Verdict in web/brief/Brief.tsx prints the app-verdict-models line and a details element with the differing models' wind and sea readings, so that part is not new. What the brief lacks is skill: how often the forecast was close to a buoy reading over the last 7 and 30 days is only on the separate /skill page (docs/roadmap.md, dist/skill-page.js). Windy's model comparison and predictability percent drew praise (2 mentions); PredictWind lists 'Validation' and 'Forecast Replay'. The new delta is only the skill figure, or 'not enough data yet', next to the existing verdict. Effort: **small**. Data: Existing skill statistics behind /skill; no new source.
10. **MUST-HAVE: One-tap catch or blank entry from the map with position and conditions attached, private by default.** Why: Fishing Points, FishAngler and Fishbrain attach conditions to log entries automatically; SkipperCast's log needs typed positions in the account panel. Effort: **small**. Data: Existing catch-log API, geolocation, the hour's conditions.
11. **NICE-TO-HAVE: Ocean true-colour / water-clarity imagery.** Why: SatFish (1-3x/day), RipCharts (daily), FishTrack and C-MAP Premium document it; SkipperCast has GOES infrared clouds only. Effort: **medium**. Data: NOAA CoastWatch VIIRS true colour (public), cloud masks, pass time.
12. **NICE-TO-HAVE: Upwelling or sea-surface-height layer.** Why: RipCharts (altimetry daily) and SatFish (SSH, SSHA daily) document it; SkipperCast has none. Effort: **medium**. Data: CoastWatch upwelling index or altimetry products; licence check.
13. **NICE-TO-HAVE: Weather radar layer.** Why: Windy, PredictWind Basic, FishAngler VIP, Fishing Points and NOAA nowCOAST (free) show radar; SkipperCast has none. Rain matters less than wind on this coast. Effort: **small**. Data: nowCOAST or MRMS public radar tiles.
14. **NICE-TO-HAVE: Add a float-plan section to the trip plan's existing offline-notes file: boat, people aboard, departure, be-back-by and an overdue instruction.** Why: Sharing the plan already exists: the trip plan exports GPX, a share-sheet file and offline notes (docs/roadmap.md line 24, docs/web-app.md line 70). In web/planner.ts the share sheet hands over the GPX file for a chartplotter app, and the offline-notes HTML (offlineNotes in dist/trip-export.js) carries the plan name, region, planned date, waypoints, the protected-area check time and a regulations link. Neither file carries a boat description, persons aboard, departure time, expected return or what a shore contact should do if the boat is overdue. Those are the core fields of the USCG float plan form, which is left with a reliable person and not filed with the Coast Guard (https://dnr.maryland.gov/nrp/Documents/float-plan.pdf). The brief already computes a be-back-by time at the bar and the account holds a boat profile. Navionics, C-MAP, Savvy Navvy and PredictWind document live location or tracking; this item does not attempt that. Effort: **small**. Data: Saved boat profile, the brief's be-back-by time, user-typed people aboard and contact note; the user hands the file on, so SkipperCast sends nothing and stores no contact.
15. **NICE-TO-HAVE: Bring a second region to 'active' (Southern California is the plan doc's candidate).** Why: FishDope and 976-TUNA concentrate on Southern California, and Fish City's listing leads with Southern California bluefin and yellowtail; SkipperCast's SoCal package is preview. Region order is the owner's call. Effort: **large**. Data: Per-region rules, forecast, closures and habitat reviews (docs/plans/second-active-region.md).

### DO NOT BUILD

- **A public catch map or social feed.** Fishbrain's sample reviews complain of wrong catch locations (3 of 10), trolls (1) and paywalled catches (2); exposes private spots; conflicts with the no-hotspot rule.
- **A solunar or AI bite score.** 3 of 10 Fishing Points reviews say high-activity days produced nothing; Windy and Fish City explicitly do not offer one; SkipperCast keeps bite potential unknown.
- **Turn-by-turn navigation as a chartplotter replacement.** Position-freeze and pop-up complaints dominate chart-app samples (C-MAP, Navionics); liability; GPX and iNavX handoff already exist.
- **Passage routing with polars and boat-motion models.** PredictWind sells it at $249-$499 for passages; its Offshore app reviews complain of abandonment and clutter; a half-day angler needs the bar and the window.
- **Unsourced hot-spot pins or bought spot lists.** SatFish 6,000+ spots and FishDope hot-bite pins carry no date or provenance on the pages read; the 2026-10-31 retro lists this as a trap.
- **Paywalls on data or features a user already has.** Paywall friction was the most frequent complaint code: 7 mentions across 4 of 10 apps (Navionics, Fishbrain, Fishing Points, PredictWind).
- **Live charter-fleet AIS hotspots.** aisstream is internal-only and MarineCadastre is planning-only in the data-rights register; the classifier has no validation; roadmap forbids hotspots without evidence.
